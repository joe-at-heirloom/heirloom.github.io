Weller pottery marks

Weller made pottery in and near Zanesville, Ohio, for some 75 years, and marked it in several ways. Here is what each mark looks like and the years it points to.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Samuel A. Weller's pottery ran from the early 1870s to 1948 2, 3, 5, 9. Its art ware of the late 1890s and early 1900s is often impressed with a line name over WELLER, such as Louwelsa or Dickens Ware 1, 5; Sicardo lustre ware of about 1901 to 1907 is signed Sicard and Weller in the glaze itself 1, 7.

Who made Weller pottery

Samuel A. Weller started with a small pottery at Fultonham, near Zanesville, Ohio, making flowerpots and plain earthenware 2, 3, 4. The year is given as 1872 by some sources 4, 5 and as about 1873 by others 2, 3. He moved into Zanesville in the 1880s and built a brick factory there in 1890 2, 3.

The first art ware came in the mid-1890s. Weller made Lonhuda ware with William A. Long of Steubenville for about a year, then a similar brown, slip-painted ware of his own called Louwelsa 2, 3, 4. The name joins Louise, his daughter's name, with his own name and initials 1, 2. Other lines followed, among them Eocean, Aurelian, Dickens Ware and Turada 1, 4.

In 1901 Jacques Sicard, who had worked for the lustre potter Clément Massier at Golfe-Juan in France, came to Zanesville and made a metallic lustre ware for Weller called Sicardo 1, 2. Museums date their Sicardo between about 1901 and 1907 7, 9.

The pottery closed in 1948, according to the Zanesville Museum of Art and the Metropolitan Museum of Art 5, 9; the RISD Museum gives 1949 10.

Weller marks, in order

Weller used more than one kind of mark at a time, and many of its items carry no mark at all 5, 6. The line name, where there is one, is the most useful clue to the date.

A line name over Weller, impressed

About 1896 to 1905

The early art lines were stamped into the clay with the line's name, often in an arc, over WELLER in capitals: Louwelsa, Dickens Ware, Turada 1, 5, 6. Barber also printed Eosian and Aurelian marks with the line name in script over WELLER 1. A number impressed with the mark, such as 525, is a shape or size number, not a date 5, 6. Louwelsa dates from 1896 at the earliest 4, 5.

A line name written by hand

About 1898 to 1910

Some early lines carry the line name and Weller written by hand into the base instead of a stamp, for example Eocean and Auroral 1, 5.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Sicard and Weller in the lustre

About 1901 to 1907

Sicardo ware is usually signed in the lustre glaze itself, low on the side rather than on the base: Sicard, Weller, or both 1, 7, 8. The base may have only an impressed number, or a small Weller mark 7, 8. Barber also printed a stamped Sicardo over Weller mark in 1904 1.

Look-alike marks to be careful with

  • Lonhuda is not Weller. Lonhuda Faience of Steubenville had its own mark, a shield with LF and the name; Weller made Lonhuda ware for about a year and bought the Lonhuda works in 1896, and Louwelsa looks very like it 1, 2, 4.
  • French lustre. Sicard came from Clément Massier's pottery in France, and Massier's lustre ware can look like Sicardo; Massier marked his ware with his own name 1, 2, 5.
  • Zanesville look-alikes. Roseville's Rozane and J. B. Owens' Utopian were brown, slip-painted wares made in the same town at the same time as Louwelsa 1, 2, 4. Read the mark rather than the look.
  • We found no museum, government or news record of modern copies carrying a Weller mark. That is not proof that none exist.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • When Weller started: 1872 4, 5 or about 1873 2, 3. The Met dates the firm from 1888, the year it moved into its Zanesville plant 9.
  • When Weller closed: 1948 5, 9 or 1949, the year the RISD Museum records 10.

Questions

How old is my Weller pottery?

Weller made pottery from the early 1870s to 1948 2, 5. A line name such as Louwelsa or Dickens Ware impressed over WELLER points to about 1896 to 1905, and Sicard written in the lustre to about 1901 to 1907 1, 5, 7.

What does Louwelsa mean?

It is a made-up name: Louise, Weller's daughter, joined with Weller and his initials, S. A. 1, 2

Where is the mark on Sicardo?

Usually in the lustre glaze low on the side, not on the base: Sicard, Weller or both, written into the decoration 7, 8.

My Weller item has no mark. Is that unusual?

No. Museum collections include Weller items with no mark at all 5, 6, and a missing mark tells you nothing on its own.
