Weller pottery marks

Weller made pottery in and near Zanesville, Ohio, for some 75 years, and marked it in several ways. Here is what each mark looks like and the years it points to.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Samuel A. Weller's pottery ran from 1872 to 1948 5, 6, 9. Its art ware of the late 1890s and early 1900s is often impressed with a line name over WELLER, such as Louwelsa or Dickens Ware 1, 8, 9; Sicardo lustre ware of 1902 to 1907 is signed Sicard and Weller in the glaze 6, 7, 11; ink kiln stamps belong to the 1920s 7, 8; and the name in raised script, sometimes with Since 1872, to the 1930s and 1940s 6, 7, 8.

Who made Weller pottery

Samuel A. Weller started with a small pottery at Fultonham, near Zanesville, Ohio, making flowerpots and plain earthenware 2, 3, 6. Most sources give 1872 4, 5, 6, 7; two local histories say about 1873 2, 3. He moved into Zanesville in the 1880s and built a brick factory there in 1890 2, 3.

The first art ware came in the mid-1890s. Weller made Lonhuda ware with William A. Long of Steubenville for about a year, then a similar brown, slip-painted ware of his own called Louwelsa 2, 3, 4. The name joins Louise, his daughter's name, with his own name and initials 1, 2. Other lines followed, among them Eocean, Aurelian, Dickens Ware and Turada 1, 4. Weller made souvenirs at the 1904 world's fair in St. Louis 2, 6, 8.

Jacques Sicard, who had worked for the lustre potter Clément Massier at Golfe-Juan in France, made a metallic lustre ware for Weller called Sicardo until he went back to France in 1907 1, 2, 6, 8.

Weller incorporated the firm in 1922 6, 8 and died on October 4, 1925 6, 7, 8. The Essex Wire Corporation of Detroit leased part of the works from 1945 and bought control in 1947; pottery making stopped in 1948 and the company was dissolved in 1949 5, 6, 7, 8.

Weller marks, in order

Weller used more than one kind of mark at a time, and many of its items carry no mark at all 9, 10. The line name, where there is one, is the most useful clue to the date.

A line name over Weller, impressed

About 1896 to 1910

The early art lines were stamped into the clay with the line's name, often in a half circle, over WELLER in capitals: Louwelsa, Dickens Ware, Turada 1, 8, 9, 10. Huxford dates these half-circle seals from 1896 to near the end of the 1900s decade 8. Barber also printed Eosian and Aurelian marks with the line name in script over WELLER 1. A number impressed with the mark, such as 525, is a shape or size number, not a date 9, 10.

A line name written by hand

About 1898 to 1910

Some early lines carry the line name and Weller incised by hand in the base instead of a stamp, for example Eocean, Aurelian and Auroral 1, 7, 9.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Sicard and Weller in the lustre

1902 to 1907

Sicardo ware is usually signed in the lustre glaze itself, low on the side or on the shoulder rather than on the base: Sicard, Weller, or both 5, 11, 12, 13. The base may have only an impressed number, a small Weller mark, or nothing 11, 12, 13. Barber also printed a stamped Sicardo over Weller mark in 1904 1.

Weller impressed in capitals

Before about 1920

WELLER alone, impressed in the base in block capitals, in larger and smaller sizes, on lines made before about 1920 5, 8, 9. The Cooper Hewitt records WELLER impressed in a partial circle on a vase it dates 1910 to 1920 13. After about 1915 the cast ware usually carries only the Weller name, incised, stamped or in the mold 5, 8.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

The kiln ink stamps

The mid to late 1920s

Weller Ware inside the outline of a kiln, or Weller Pottery in a half-kiln outline, stamped in ink on the base 7, 8, 9.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Paper and foil labels

The 1920s to the 1940s

Labels with Weller Pottery or a line name, such as Glendale or Velvo; Huxford puts them in the 1920s, Lehner in the 1930s and 1940s 5, 8, 9. Once the label is gone, the item is unmarked.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Weller in script, by hand or in the mold

About 1930 to 1948

Weller or Weller Pottery written by hand in the base, used in the 1930s and 1940s 5, 9; and the same name in raised script cast in the mold, from about 1933 to 1935 until the end, often with Since 1872 below 6, 7, 8, 9. A Henry Ford record dates a vase inscribed Weller Pottery to 1890 to 1900, much earlier than the books 10.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Look-alike marks to be careful with

  • Lonhuda is not Weller. Lonhuda Faience of Steubenville had its own mark, a shield with LF and the name; Weller made Lonhuda ware for about a year and bought the Lonhuda works in 1896, and Louwelsa looks very like it 1, 2, 4.
  • French lustre. Sicard came from Clément Massier's pottery in France, and Massier's lustre ware can look like Sicardo; Massier marked his ware with his own name 1, 2, 9.
  • Zanesville look-alikes. Roseville's Rozane and J. B. Owens' Utopian were brown, slip-painted wares made in the same town at the same time as Louwelsa 1, 2, 4. Read the mark rather than the look.
  • Unmarked Weller. Museum collections include Weller items with no mark at all, and labels have often come off 5, 9, 10.
  • We found no museum, government or news record of modern copies carrying a Weller mark, and only one book mentions copies. That is not proof that none exist.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • When Weller started: 1872 4, 5, 6, 7 or about 1873 2, 3. The Met dates the firm from 1888, the year it moved into its Zanesville plant 14.
  • When Weller closed: pottery making stopped in 1948 and the company was dissolved in 1949 6, which is why museums give either year 9, 14, 15.
  • When it incorporated: 1922 6, 8, or 1925 in the Kovels' book 7.

Questions

How old is my Weller pottery?

Weller made pottery from 1872 to 1948 5, 6. A line name such as Louwelsa impressed over WELLER points to about 1896 to 1910; Sicard in the lustre to 1902 to 1907; a kiln ink stamp to the 1920s; Weller in raised script, often with Since 1872, to the 1930s and 1940s 1, 7, 8, 9.

What does Louwelsa mean?

It is a made-up name: Louise, Weller's daughter, joined with Weller and his initials, S. A. 1, 2

Where is the mark on Sicardo?

Usually in the lustre glaze on the side or shoulder, not on the base: Sicard, Weller or both 11, 12, 13.

What does Weller Pottery Since 1872 mean?

It is the molded script mark of Weller's last years, from the 1930s to 1948; 1872 is the year the pottery began, not the year the item was made 6, 7, 8.

My Weller item has no mark. Is that unusual?

No. Museum collections include Weller items with no mark at all, and many labels have come off 5, 9, 10.
