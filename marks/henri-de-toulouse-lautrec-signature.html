Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec's signature, 1880 to 1899

13 close-ups of Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec's signature (French, 1864–1901), from works dated 1880–1899, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

As a teenager he signed with his HTL monogram and 'Monfa', from the family name Toulouse-Lautrec-Monfa, as on The Trap (1880).

How Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec signed

  • From the late 1880s his usual painted signature joins the initials H and T in a ligature and continues with the surname, read 'HTLautrec' or 'THLautrec'.
  • In his lithographs the signature is often an HTL monogram in a circle drawn on the stone, sometimes with the year, as on the Jane Avril poster of 1899.
  • Many individual impressions are also signed in pencil below the image, 'THLautrec', sometimes with a number.
  • Works from his studio carry a red 'HTL' estate stamp in a circle (Lugt 1338).

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec sign paintings?

As a teenager he signed with his HTL monogram and 'Monfa', from the family name Toulouse-Lautrec-Monfa, as on The Trap (1880).

Did Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec always sign the same way?

No. The 13 examples on this page, from 1880–1899, show atelier stamp (Lugt 1338), estate stamp, initials (graphite), initials in ligature and surname. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
