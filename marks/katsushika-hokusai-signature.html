Katsushika Hokusai's signature, 1799 to 1835

8 close-ups of Katsushika Hokusai's signature (Japanese, 1760–1849), from works dated 1799–1835, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Hokusai was known by at least thirty names during his lifetime.

How Katsushika Hokusai signed

  • From 1779, at nineteen, he worked in the Katsukawa studio for fourteen years under the name Shunrō, given to him by his master Shunshō.
  • After leaving the Katsukawa school in 1793 he worked independently under the names Sōri and Kakō; an early painting of 1798 is signed 'Hokusai Sōri ga'.
  • His signatures can record the change of name itself: a 1799 surimono in Cleveland is signed 'Sōri aratame Hokusai ga', aratame meaning 'changing (name) to'.
  • On his One Hundred Poems prints of 1835–36 he signed 'Zen Hokusai' (前北斎, 'former Hokusai') with a seal reading Manji (卍).

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Katsushika Hokusai sign paintings?

Hokusai was known by at least thirty names during his lifetime.

Did Katsushika Hokusai always sign the same way?

No. The 8 examples on this page, from 1799–1835, show 'former Hokusai' with Manji seal, art name change: Hokusai to Iitsu, art name change: Hokusai to Iitsu, with seal, art name change: Hokusai to Taito. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Katsushika Hokusai?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Katsushika Hokusai is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
