Xianfeng mark 咸豐

The Xianfeng reign of the Qing dynasty ran 1851–1861 (some tables to 1860). Its mark reads 大清咸豐年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Marked Xianfeng items are scarce: Taiping forces took Jingdezhen and destroyed the imperial kiln in the 1850s (1853, the third year of the reign, per Hobson and the Chinese record; Bushell gives the sixth year), and it was not rebuilt until 1866. From this reign on, regular script is again the usual imperial mark, replacing the seal script of Qianlong to Daoguang. From this reign on, regular script is again the usual imperial mark, replacing the seal script of Qianlong to Daoguang.1, 2, 3, 4 No common later copies are recorded in the sources.4

The Xianfeng mark

  • 大清咸豐年製 · 6 characters, regular script. Bushell: imperial items usually marked in full, penciled in red in regular script.
  • 大清咸豐年製 · 6 characters, seal script. Less common than regular script.
2, 4, 5, 6, 7

Years: 1851–1861 (some tables to 1860). 1851-1861 standard. Bushell's Chinese Art gives 1851-60; Gulland 1851-1862.2, 6, 7, 8, 9

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Hsien-feng, Hsien Feng, Heen-fung (Gulland).

Where the reign’s name appears

Besides its marks, the reign’s name is written on other dated things: coins, inscriptions and dedications.

Later items with this mark

None documented.4

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
