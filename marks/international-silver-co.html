International Silver Co. marks

International Silver made both sterling and plate, under its own name and a dozen older ones. Here is how to tell which you have, and how to date it.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The International Silver Co. was formed in Meriden, Connecticut, on November 19, 1898, from a dozen silver firms, and kept many of their names, including 1847 Rogers Bros., Holmes & Edwards and Wm. Rogers. It made both sterling and plate: only a mark that says STERLING is sterling. Date flatware from its pattern; we found no year code on its hollowware.

Who made it

The International Silver Co. was organized on November 19, 1898, under New Jersey law, with its head office in Meriden, Connecticut. It began by buying a dozen New England silver firms: Barbour Silver Co., Holmes & Edwards Silver Co., Manhattan Silver Plate Co., Meriden Britannia Co., Meriden Silver Plate Co., Norwich Cutlery Co., Wm. Rogers Mfg. Co., Rogers Cutlery Co., Rogers & Bro., Rogers & Hamilton Co., Wilcox Silver Plate Co., Watrous Mfg. Co. and Standard Silver Co. of Toronto. Simpson, Hall, Miller & Co., the Derby Silver Co. and two others followed in 1899, and C. Rogers & Bros. in 1903.

It became one of the largest American makers of silverware, in both sterling and plate. The parent company renamed itself Insilco in 1969, the silverware business was sold to Katy Industries in 1983 or 1984 (the sources differ), and the International Silver name reached Lifetime Brands with its purchase of Syratech in 2006.

International kept many of its founding firms' names as brands, so much International ware doesn't say International at all: see 1847 Rogers Bros., Holmes & Edwards and Wm. Rogers.

Is it plate or sterling?

It can be either, which is why the whole mark matters. International made sterling under its own name and some of its brands, and a great deal of plate. A spoon in a Canadian parliamentary collection, for example, is marked INTERNATIONAL S. CO. XII and is electroplated nickel silver. If the mark says STERLING, it is sterling; if not, treat it as plate.

Read the whole stamp first. American sterling is marked STERLING. Plate is marked with the maker's name and often a grade word or number, such as A1, XII, XIV, TRIPLE or QUADRUPLE PLATE, and a grade mark appears only on plate.

Then look at the places that wear. Plate is a thin coat of silver over a base metal, usually nickel silver, so on a well-used spoon the tip of the bowl, the back of the bowl and the ends of fork tines turn yellowish or gray where the silver has worn through to the metal underneath.

How to date International Silver

American plate makers didn't stamp a year. A number in the mark, such as 1847 or 1865, is the year a firm said it was founded and stayed on the ware for decades. To date an item, name its pattern (the design of the handle) and find when that pattern was introduced. A patent date stamped on a handle (PAT. followed by a date) is the earliest the design can be, not the year the item was made, and makers kept producing a popular pattern for decades.

We found no published year-code system for International's hollowware, so don't read a number on the base of a tray or teapot as a date; it is usually a catalog or pattern number. Questions

Is International Silver sterling?

Sometimes. International made both sterling and silver plate. An item marked STERLING, such as INTERNATIONAL STERLING, is sterling; one with a grade mark, or with no STERLING in the mark, is plate.

When was International Silver founded?

On November 19, 1898, in Meriden, Connecticut, by buying a dozen New England silver companies, with more added in 1899 and 1903.

Is 1847 Rogers Bros. International Silver?

From 1898, yes. Meriden Britannia, which made 1847 Rogers Bros., was one of International's founding firms, and International kept the brand. Holmes & Edwards and Wm. Rogers came in the same way.

Does a number on International hollowware date it?

We found no published year code for International hollowware. A number on the base is most often a catalog or pattern number.

Who owns International Silver now?

The name passed through Katy Industries, Wallace International and Syratech to Lifetime Brands, which bought Syratech in 2006.
