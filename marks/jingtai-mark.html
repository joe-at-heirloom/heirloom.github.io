Jingtai mark 景泰

The Jingtai reign of the Ming dynasty ran 1450–1456 (some tables to 1457). No reign mark of it is recorded on imperial porcelain. Here is what is known, and where its name does appear.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Part of the mid-15th-century 'interregnum' or 'blank period' (空白期) of Zhengtong, Jingtai and Tianshun: official kiln wares of these reigns carry no reign marks. Records suggest reduced output rather than none, and excavations at the Jingdezhen imperial kiln site since 1988 have found official wares of the period, all unmarked.1, 2, 3, 4 No common later copies are recorded in the sources.1, 2

The Jingtai mark

  • No mark of this reign is recorded on imperial porcelain; the photographs below show where its name does appear.
1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8

Years: 1450–1456 (some tables to 1457). Hobson (1915) and Bushell's Chinese Art give 1450-1456; Wikipedia, Chinese Wikipedia, UCSD, Gulland and Jiang give 1450-1457, because the era ran into early 1457 (to 14 February 1457) before Tianshun began.2, 3, 7, 8

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Ching-t'ai, King-tai (Gulland).

Later items with this mark

None documented as a common copied mark. The Jingtai era name is well known on cloisonné enamel (景泰藍), not on porcelain.1, 2

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
