Rembrandt van Rijn's signature, 1626 to 1659

14 close-ups of Rembrandt van Rijn's signature (Dutch, 1606–1669), from works dated 1626–1659, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Rembrandt's earliest signed painting, dated 1625, carries a capital R; he then added an H, sometimes with an f for fecit ('made').

How Rembrandt van Rijn signed

  • In 1628 he added an L, read as Leydensis ('from Leiden'), giving the RHL monogram; he abandoned the L in the course of 1632.
  • During 1632 he began adding 'van Rijn' to the monogram, and in 1632–33 he switched to signing with his first name alone in cursive script.
  • The first-name signature was at first spelled ending in -nt ('Rembrant') and later -ndt ('Rembrandt'); he signed with his first name only until his death in 1669.
  • About three quarters of Rembrandt's known paintings bear his name; a 2017 survey by Ernst van de Wetering counted 257 signed works among 349 paintings.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Rembrandt van Rijn sign paintings?

Rembrandt's earliest signed painting, dated 1625, carries a capital R; he then added an H, sometimes with an f for fecit ('made').

Did Rembrandt van Rijn always sign the same way?

No. The 14 examples on this page, from 1626–1659, show first name and date, first name, f(ecit) and date, inscribed: first name with 'geretuck' (retouched) and date, monogram and date. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Rembrandt van Rijn?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Rembrandt van Rijn is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
