Marks and serial numbers

Many things carry a small mark that dates them: a silver hallmark's letter, a sewing machine's serial, a watch movement's number. These pages turn the mark into a year, from the same published tables Heirloom Advisor uses.

A full set of marks on a silver tankard made in Exeter: maker, leopard's head, lion passant, the Exeter town mark and a date letter. Photograph: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access (public domain), cut out and toned by Heirloom.

How these tables were made

Every table was compiled from at least two published sources and checked row by row. Where the sources disagree, the page shows both years rather than choosing one. Nothing is filled in between rows. Each page lists its sources and the cautions that come with them.

A mark dates an item; it does not prove what the item is. Marks can be worn, copied or added later, and Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine.
