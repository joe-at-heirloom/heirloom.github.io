Community Plate and Oneida marks

Community Plate was Oneida's best silver plate from 1901–02. Here is what each Oneida mark tells you, and how to date yours.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Community and Community Plate are silver-plated flatware made by Oneida Community Ltd. (Oneida Ltd. from 1935), starting with the Avalon pattern in 1901–02. It is plate, not sterling; Oneida's sterling says STERLING. Date it from its pattern: Morning Star is 1948, Evening Star 1950.

Who made it

The Oneida Community was a religious commune founded by John Humphrey Noyes at Oneida, New York, in 1848. Its branch at Wallingford, Connecticut, began making spoons in 1877; the company's 1901 booklet calls them silver plate, while its 1948 history and a Connecticut history say they were tinned iron.

In 1880 the commune became a company, Oneida Community Ltd. (some accounts date the change from January 1, 1881), and the spoon works moved to Niagara Falls. Its first Community Plate pattern, Avalon, was shown at the end of 1901 and went on sale in 1902, in a single heavy grade of plate. The company bought Wm. A. Rogers Ltd. in 1929, took the name Oneida Ltd. on March 1, 1935, and stopped making flatware in the United States in 2005.

We haven't yet found an openly licensed photograph of a Community mark; it is stamped on the back of the handle.

Is it plate or sterling?

Community and Community Plate are silver plate: a coat of silver over a base metal. Oneida also made sterling, and those items say STERLING in the mark.

Read the whole stamp first. American sterling is marked STERLING. Plate is marked with the maker's name and often a grade word or number, such as A1, XII, XIV, TRIPLE or QUADRUPLE PLATE, and a grade mark appears only on plate.

Then look at the places that wear. Plate is a thin coat of silver over a base metal, usually nickel silver, so on a well-used spoon the tip of the bowl, the back of the bowl and the ends of fork tines turn yellowish or gray where the silver has worn through to the metal underneath.

How to date Community Plate

American plate makers didn't stamp a year. A number in the mark, such as 1847 or 1865, is the year a firm said it was founded and stayed on the ware for decades. To date an item, name its pattern (the design of the handle) and find when that pattern was introduced. A patent date stamped on a handle (PAT. followed by a date) is the earliest the design can be, not the year the item was made, and makers kept producing a popular pattern for decades.

The Community line runs from 1901–02, so no Community Plate is older than that, and ONEIDA LTD. in the mark means 1935 or later. These introduction years are the ones two published sources agree on:

PatternIntroduced
AvalonShown 1901, on sale 1902 (one guide says about 1903)

Years for many other Community patterns (Patrician, Adam, Bird of Paradise, Coronation, White Orchid and more) are in one published guide and on collectors' sites, but we haven't found a second independent source, so they aren't listed. For South Seas the sources disagree: 1955 in one guide, 1953 on collectors' sites.

Questions

Is Community Plate real silver?

It is silver plate: a coat of real silver over a base-metal blank. It is not sterling. Oneida sold it as heavier than ordinary triple plate.

How old is my Oneida Community silverware?

Community Plate began with the Avalon pattern in 1901–02, so it is no older than that. Find the pattern and its introduction year; ONEIDA LTD. in the mark means 1935 or later.

What is the difference between Community, Tudor Plate, Reliance and Par Plate?

All are Oneida silver plate. Community was the top grade; Reliance, Tudor Plate and Par Plate were cheaper lines.

Did Oneida make sterling?

Yes, marked Oneida Community Sterling and later Heirloom Sterling. An item is sterling only if the mark says STERLING.

Is Heirloom Sterling connected with the Heirloom app?

No. Heirloom Sterling is Oneida's sterling flatware line, a brand Oneida acquired in 1929. Heirloom, the app, has no connection with it.
