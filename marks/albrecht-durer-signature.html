Albrecht Dürer's signature, 1495 to 1526

13 close-ups of Albrecht Dürer's signature (German, 1471–1528), from works dated 1495–1526, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

On Adam and Eve (1504) Dürer put his full Latin name on a hanging tablet: 'ALBERT DVRER NORICVS FACIEBAT', followed by the AD monogram and the date.

How Albrecht Dürer signed

  • Marcantonio Raimondi engraved copies of Dürer's prints around 1506–1508, duplicating Dürer's monogram as well.
  • Vasari reports that Dürer went to Venice to petition the Senate against Marcantonio's copies, but the story may be apocryphal: the Senate archives contain no mention of the petition.
  • Dürer's monogram was itself added to works by later hands: the National Gallery of Art records a false monogram added beside the artist's own inscription on his 1504 drawing The Entombment.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Albrecht Dürer sign paintings?

On Adam and Eve (1504) Dürer put his full Latin name on a hanging tablet: 'ALBERT DVRER NORICVS FACIEBAT', followed by the AD monogram and the date.

Did Albrecht Dürer always sign the same way?

No. The 13 examples on this page, from 1495–1526, show inscribed tablet: full Latin name, monogram and date, inscribed tablet: name in Latin, Roman-numeral date and monogram, monogram, monogram and date. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Albrecht Dürer?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Albrecht Dürer is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
