Roseville pottery marks

Roseville marked its pottery with a seal, then an ink stamp, then labels, then a script name in the mold. Each tells you roughly when an item was made.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The Roseville Pottery Company began in Roseville, Ohio, in 1890, moved to Zanesville in 1898 and closed in 1954 1, 2, 6. A round Rozane Ware seal points to about 1904 to 1907, an Rv ink stamp to about 1916 to 1925, and the raised script “Roseville U.S.A.” with a shape number to the mid-1930s through 1954 4, 7, 8.

Who made Roseville pottery

The Roseville Pottery Company began in 1890 in Roseville, Ohio, and was incorporated in 1892 1, 2, 3. It made flowerpots, stoneware and plain jardinieres from local clay 1, 2, 3.

In 1898 it bought the Clark Stoneware plant on Linden Avenue in Zanesville and moved there 1, 2, 3. Its first art ware, Rozane, was developed there in 1900 and 1901: brown ware painted with colored slips under the glaze, like the Louwelsa of its neighbor Weller 3, 4. The company's own 1905 catalog shows Rozane Royal and the lines that followed it, among them Mongol, Egypto, Mara and Woodland 5.

The company closed in 1954 1, 6, 12. Since 2017 the Roseville names have been registered trademarks of a company in Indiana that sells new items under them 12, 13; those are not products of the original pottery.

Roseville marks, in order

Roseville's marks overlap at their edges, and many of its items left the factory with only a paper or foil label, so a missing mark is common 7, 9.

The Rozane seal

About 1904 to 1907

A circle with a rose at the upper right and the words ROZANE WARE across it 4, 5. Barber printed the seal in 1904 and the company's 1905 catalog carries it on its pages 4, 5.

The Rv ink stamp

About 1916 to 1925

A small monogram of a capital R with a small v, stamped in ink on the base, often in blue. Museums record it on items they date from 1916 to about 1924 7, 8.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Paper and foil labels

Mostly the later 1920s and early 1930s

Many items of these years were marked only with a stuck-on label, the later ones silver foil reading Roseville Pottery 7, 9. Once the label is gone, the item is unmarked 7, 9.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Roseville in script, impressed

The mid-1930s

The name in script sunk into the base from the mold, with a shape number. The Zanesville Museum of Art records it on lines of about 1935 to 1937 7. The Henry Ford describes an impressed “U.S.A. / Roseville” mark on a vase it dates 1945 to 1950, so this form was not confined to the 1930s 8.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Roseville U.S.A. in raised script

The mid-1930s to 1954

The name in script raised from the base, with U.S.A. below it and a shape number with a size in inches, such as 201-7 7, 8. Museums record it on lines from the mid-1930s to the last designs of the early 1950s 7, 8.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Reproductions and later marks to be careful with

  • Copies of Roseville shapes made in China have been sold in the US since the 1980s, some with a raised Roseville mark molded in 9, 10, 11.
  • On the museum examples, the raised script mark has U.S.A. under the name 7, 8. A newspaper column described a reproduction whose raised mark had no U.S.A., with weaker glaze and modeling than the old ware 10. A missing or faint U.S.A. is a reason to look closer, not a verdict.
  • New items have been sold under the Roseville trademarks since 2017 12, 13. They are marked as new products, not as old ones.
  • Other potteries in the town of Roseville, Ohio, such as Robinson-Ransbottom, put the town's name in their marks 7, 11. A town name is not the Roseville Pottery Company's mark.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • The start year is given as 1890 1, 7 or 1892 2, 6: 1890 is when the company began and 1892 when it was incorporated 1, 3.
  • Barber printed the round Rozane Ware mark in 1904 4; the Zanesville Museum of Art dates the applied seal on the ware from 1905 or 1906 7.

Questions

How do I date Roseville pottery by the mark?

The round Rozane Ware seal points to about 1904 to 1907; the Rv ink stamp to about 1916 to 1925; a foil or paper label alone to the later 1920s or early 1930s; and the raised script “Roseville U.S.A.” with a shape number to the mid-1930s through 1954 4, 7, 8, 9.

When did Roseville close?

In 1954 1, 6.

My Roseville has raised letters but no U.S.A. What does that mean?

The raised marks in museum collections have U.S.A. under the name 7, 8, and reproductions have been reported without it 10. It is a reason to look closer; Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine.

Why does my Roseville have no mark?

Many items of the later 1920s and early 1930s carried only a paper or foil label, and most labels have long since come off 7, 9.

What is Rozane?

Roseville's first art ware, developed in 1900 and 1901, and the name of a family of lines that followed it 3, 5.
