Roseville pottery marks

Roseville marked its pottery with a seal, then an ink stamp, then labels, then a script name in the mold. Each tells you roughly when an item was made.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The Roseville Pottery Company began in Roseville, Ohio, in 1890, moved to Zanesville in 1898 and closed in 1954 1, 2, 6. The round Rozane Ware seal points to about 1904 to 1906, the Rv ink stamp to about 1910 to 1928, foil labels to the 1930s, and the raised script “Roseville U.S.A.” with a shape number to the mid-1930s through 1954 6, 8, 10, 12.

Who made Roseville pottery

The Roseville Pottery Company began in 1890 in Roseville, Ohio, and was incorporated in 1892 1, 2, 7, 10. It made flowerpots, stoneware and plain jardinieres from local clay 1, 2, 3.

In 1898 it bought the Clark Stoneware plant on Linden Avenue in Zanesville and moved there; from 1917 all its pottery was made in Zanesville 1, 2, 3, 6, 8. Its first art ware, Rozane, was developed there in 1900 and 1901: brown ware painted with colored slips under the glaze, like the Louwelsa of its neighbor Weller 3, 4. The name was made from Roseville and Zanesville 8, 9. The company's own 1905 catalog shows Rozane Royal and the lines that followed it, among them Mongol, Egypto, Mara and Woodland 5.

The pottery closed on November 1, 1954, and the plant was sold to the Mosaic Tile Company that month 1, 6, 8. Since 2017 the Roseville names have been registered trademarks of a company in Indiana that sells new items under them 16, 17; those are not products of the original pottery.

Roseville marks, in order

Roseville's marks overlap at their edges, and many of its items left the factory with only a paper or foil label, so a missing mark is common 6, 12, 14.

Rozane and RPCo, impressed

About 1900 to 1904

Rozane, RPCo (for Roseville Pottery Company) and a shape number, stamped into the base of the early Rozane ware 7, 8, 12. Some early items carry RPCo alone 6, 8.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

The Rozane seal

About 1904 to 1906

A circle with a rose and the words ROZANE WARE across it, on a small disc applied to the base, sometimes with the line's name, such as Mongol or Egypto 6, 7, 8, 12. Barber printed the seal in 1904, and the company's 1905 catalog carries it on its pages 4, 5.

Rozane Olympic, in black

About 1905

Rozane Olympic Pottery in black on the base, often with the title of the scene painted on the item 6, 8, 10, 12.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

The Rv ink stamp

About 1910 to 1928

A monogram of a capital R with a small v in its loop, usually stamped in ink on the base, sometimes impressed, in a small and a large size 8, 10. Museums record it on items they date from 1916 to about 1924 12, 13.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Donatello and R.P.Co.

From 1915

The Donatello line of 1915 was often unmarked, but some items carry Donatello and R.P.Co. pressed into the base 8, 10, 12.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Paper and foil labels

About 1914 to 1937

Many items of these years were marked only with a stuck-on label: a black paper label from about 1914, then silver or gold foil from 1930 6, 8, 12. Once the label is gone, the item is unmarked 12, 14.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Roseville in script, impressed

About 1932 to 1939

The name in script sunk into the base from the mold, with a shape number 6, 8, 12. The Henry Ford describes an impressed U.S.A. Roseville mark on a vase it dates 1945 to 1950, so the form was not confined to the 1930s 13.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Roseville U.S.A. in raised script

The mid-1930s to 1954

The name in script raised from the base, with U.S.A. below it and a shape number with a size in inches, such as 201-7 6, 8, 10, 12, 13. The books date its start differently, from 1935 to 1939 (see below) 6, 8, 10.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Reproductions and later marks to be careful with

  • Copies of Roseville shapes made in China have been sold in the US since the 1980s or 1990s, most with a copy of the raised Roseville mark molded in, some sold with a removable Made in China label 9, 10, 14, 15.
  • On the old ware, the raised script mark has U.S.A. under the name 12, 13, 15. Copies have been reported with the name but no U.S.A., or a faint one 8, 15. A missing U.S.A. is a reason to look closer, not a verdict.
  • New items have been sold under the Roseville trademarks since 2017 16, 17. They are marked as new products, not as old ones.
  • Other potteries in the town of Roseville, Ohio, such as Robinson-Ransbottom, put the town's name in their marks 6, 10. A town name is not the Roseville Pottery Company's mark.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • The start year is given as 1890 1, 7 or 1892 10, 11: 1890 is when the company began and 1892 when it was incorporated 1, 3.
  • The Rozane seal: 1904 in the books 6, 7, 8, but 1905 or 1906 in the Zanesville Museum of Art's records 12.
  • The raised script mark: on some items after 1931 and most from 1939 according to Lehner 6; from 1935 according to the Kovels 10; from 1937 according to Huxford 8.
  • The Donatello mark: impressed according to the Kovels and Huxford 8, 10, molded according to the Zanesville Museum of Art 12.

Questions

How do I date Roseville pottery by the mark?

Rozane and RPCo impressed points to about 1900 to 1904; the round Rozane Ware seal to about 1904 to 1906; the Rv ink stamp to about 1910 to 1928; a foil label alone to the 1930s; impressed script to the 1930s; and raised script “Roseville U.S.A.” with a shape number to the mid-1930s through 1954 6, 8, 10, 12.

When did Roseville close?

On November 1, 1954; the plant was sold to the Mosaic Tile Company that month 1, 6, 8.

My Roseville has raised letters but no U.S.A. What does that mean?

The raised marks on the old ware have U.S.A. under the name 12, 13, and reproductions have been reported without it 8, 15. It is a reason to look closer; Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine.

Why does my Roseville have no mark?

Many items of the 1910s to the 1930s carried only a paper or foil label, and most labels have long since come off 6, 8, 14.

What is Rozane?

Roseville's first art ware, developed in 1900 and 1901, named from Roseville and Zanesville, and the name of a family of lines that followed it 3, 5, 8, 9.
