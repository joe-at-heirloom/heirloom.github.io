Childe Hassam's signature, 1885 to 1926

11 close-ups of Childe Hassam's signature (American, 1859–1935), from works dated 1885–1926, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Hassam's early signatures carry a crescent before the name; later he reduced it to a simple slash.

How Childe Hassam signed

  • In his pen drawings of 1885–86 he stacked the signature in three lines: 'Childe / Hassam / 85'.
  • From 1918 his lithographs are signed and dated on the stone, often with the day and place, and each impression is also marked in pencil with his monogram.
  • His late etchings carry the monogram in the plate; the pencil monogram is followed by 'imp' to show he printed the impression himself.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Childe Hassam sign paintings?

Hassam's early signatures carry a crescent before the name; later he reduced it to a simple slash.

Did Childe Hassam always sign the same way?

No. The 11 examples on this page, from 1885–1926, show crescent stroke, full name and date, crescent, full name stacked, short date, crescent, full name, place and date on the stone; monogram in pencil, full name and date. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Childe Hassam?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Childe Hassam is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
