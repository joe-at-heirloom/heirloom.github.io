Christofle marks

Christofle made silver plate from 1842 and solid silver beside it. Its marks tell the two apart, and roughly date them.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Most Christofle is silver plate, marked with a square maker's mark and the name CHRISTOFLE; solid silver also carries the French Minerva head. Christofle says its maker's mark shows a balance with CC until 1935 and OC after, and that a knight with OC marks the quality of plate from 1935.

The firm

Christofle dates itself to 1830. Charles Christofle (1805–1863) had become a partner in his brother-in-law's Paris jewelry firm in 1825 and ran it alone from 1831. From 1842 he bought the French patents for electroplating with silver and gold, first Henri de Ruolz's and then Elkington's, which came earlier, and built the business on silver plate: a thin coat of silver laid by electrolysis on a base metal. The firm also made solid silver, and its large works were at Saint-Denis, north of Paris.

Christofle silver plate

Most Christofle is silver plate. Under French law, makers of plated goods had to strike a square maker's mark, not the diamond-shaped lozenge of solid silver; a decree of 1860 extended this to electroplate. So Christofle plate has a square maker's mark, the name, and no Minerva head.

Christofle also promised the weight of silver it put on. In his note to the jury of the 1849 exhibition, Charles Christofle wrote that the firm stood behind the amount of silver deposited, with its mark and name struck on all its wares, and the 1851 jury reported that his punch gave in figures the grams of silver on each item, or on each dozen spoons and forks. By 1910, a legal writer noted, electroplaters in general no longer stated the weight, so a number on later Christofle should not be read as grams.

What Christofle says its marks show

The details below come from Christofle's own guide and history. No independent source confirming them was found, so take them as the company's account:

  • The maker's mark: a balance (a pair of scales), a bee, stars, laurel branches and two letters: CC for Charles Christofle, then OC for Orfèvrerie Christofle from 1935.
  • The quality mark, from 1935: a chess knight with OC, for the thickness of the silver or gold plate; from 1983 with a Roman numeral, I or II, for first or second quality of plate.
  • Name stamps: CHRISTOFLE in a rectangle, plain or in script, CHRISTOFLE FRANCE, or GALLIA.
  • Gallia: a line of plate Christofle created at the end of the 19th century, marked with a cockerel over the word GALLIA.

Christofle solid silver

Solid silver by Christofle carries the French state mark, the Minerva head, with the maker's mark: on the statuette whose name stamp is shown below, Paris Musées records a Minerva and a maker's mark of CC with a bee. Silver of this kind is marked like any French silver, so the Minerva head is what tells it from plate.

Read these before you rely on a mark

  • The meaning of the balance, the CC to OC change in 1935, the knight quality mark and the Gallia cockerel rest on Christofle's own account; museums and older books found here confirm only the CC with a bee and the name stamps.
  • A number on Christofle plate was a weight of silver in the firm's early years; on later plate it may not be.
  • Christofle is widely copied and its name is often used loosely. The marks tell you what the item claims; for more, ask the company or a specialist.

Questions

Is Christofle real silver?

Most Christofle is silver plate: silver laid by electrolysis on a base metal, marked with a square maker's mark and the name. Christofle solid silver also carries the French Minerva head. If there is no Minerva head, it is plate.

What do the letters CC and OC mean on Christofle?

According to Christofle, CC (Charles Christofle) appears in its maker's mark until 1935, and OC (Orfèvrerie Christofle) from then on. That gives a rough date: OC means 1935 or later.

What is Gallia?

A line of silver plate made by Christofle from the end of the 19th century, marked GALLIA, with a cockerel in later marks, according to the company.

What does the number on old Christofle mean?

On early Christofle plate, the punch gave in figures the grams of silver deposited, on each item or on each dozen spoons and forks, as the 1851 exhibition jury reported. On later plate the number may be something else, such as a model number.
