Royal Copenhagen marks

Every Royal Copenhagen item carries the three wavy lines. What is printed around them tells you roughly when it was made, and from 1935 the backstamp carries a year code.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The Royal Porcelain Factory in Copenhagen was founded in 1775 and has marked its porcelain with three wavy blue lines ever since 1, 2, 3. A printed crown with Royal Copenhagen around it means 1889 or later 1, 2, and the wording Royal Copenhagen Denmark around the crown appears on 20th-century ware 4, 6.

Who made Royal Copenhagen

Hard-paste porcelain in Copenhagen grew out of the chemist Frantz Heinrich Müller's experiments; a company with a porcelain monopoly was formed in 1775 under the patronage of the dowager queen Juliane Marie 1, 5. Chaffers gives 1772 for the start of Müller's works 3.

The king took the factory over in 1779, and it became the Royal Porcelain Factory, Den kongelige Porcelainsfabrik 1, 3. It later passed out of royal ownership; the sources give different years (see below) 1, 3.

Under Philip Schou the works moved to Frederiksberg in 1884, with Arnold Krog as artistic director, and the factory's underglaze-painted ware made its name at the Paris exhibition of 1889 1, 2.

Royal Copenhagen marks, in order

The three waves, painted

From 1775

Three wavy lines one above another, painted in blue under the glaze, for Denmark's three waters: the Sound, the Great Belt and the Little Belt 1, 2, 3. On older ware the painted waves stand alone, often with a painter's number or mark beside them and a modeler's mark incised in the base 1, 2.

The green crown with Royal Copenhagen

From 1889

A crown inside a circle reading Royal Copenhagen, printed in green, above the blue waves. Hayden says it was used from 1889 on much of the ware for the English and American markets, and the factory wrote in 1904 that the crown had been added a few years before 1, 2. Royal Copenhagen's own backstamp chart dates this form 1889 to 1922 4.

A blue crown over the waves

From about 1905

A crown painted in blue over the three lines, with the painter's initials beside it, marks the factory's reissues of its early overglaze models of the Müller period 1, 4. A crowned mark of this kind means a reissue, not an 18th-century original 1, 4.

Royal Copenhagen Denmark around the crown

From 1923

The crown with Royal Copenhagen Denmark printed around it in green, over the blue waves: the 20th-century backstamp, recorded by the V&A on items of 1938 and 1957 4, 6.

The year code

From 1935

Royal Copenhagen's own backstamp chart gives the code 4: from 1935 to 1949 a small mark over one letter of Royal Copenhagen, in order (R for 1935, O for 1936, and so on to the last N for 1949); from 1950 a mark under one letter of Royal, Copenhagen or Denmark, again in order; and from 1985 marks over or under pairs of letters, each pair covering several years. We found no second published source for the code, so check it against the chart itself.

Reissues, look-alikes and other marks to be careful with

  • Reissues. The factory has reissued its early models since about 1905, marked with a crown over the waves rather than the waves alone 1, 4.
  • Bing & Grøndahl is a different Copenhagen factory, founded in 1853, with its own B & G mark 1, 3. Its style can be close to Royal Copenhagen's.
  • Faience. Earthenware from the Aluminia factory, which ran with Royal Copenhagen, can carry Royal Copenhagen wording; it is not porcelain 1, 2.
  • Seconds. Dealers say a line scratched or ground through the waves marks a second-quality item, but we found no published source for it, so it is not stated as fact here.
  • We found no published record of forgeries of the Royal Copenhagen mark itself.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • The start: a company formed in 1775 1, 5, or Müller's works begun in 1772 according to Chaffers 3.
  • The end of royal ownership: bought by A. Falck in 1867 according to Hayden 1; ended in 1864 according to Chaffers 3.

Questions

What do the three wavy lines on Royal Copenhagen mean?

They stand for Denmark's three waters, the Sound, the Great Belt and the Little Belt, and have been the factory's mark since 1775 1, 2, 3.

How do I date Royal Copenhagen?

Waves alone point to older ware; a printed green crown with Royal Copenhagen to 1889 or later; Royal Copenhagen Denmark around the crown to 1923 or later 1, 2, 4. From 1935 the maker's own chart decodes a year mark in the lettering 4.

What does a crown painted in blue over the waves mean?

It marks the factory's reissues of its early models, made from about 1905 1, 4.

Is Bing & Grøndahl the same as Royal Copenhagen?

No. It is a separate Copenhagen factory, founded in 1853, with its own mark 1, 3.
