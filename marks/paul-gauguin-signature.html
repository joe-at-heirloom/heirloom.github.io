Paul Gauguin's signature, 1884 to 1903

15 close-ups of Paul Gauguin's signature (French, 1848–1903), from works dated 1884–1903, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Gauguin often wrote a dedication above his signature on works given to friends, as on Still Life with Peonies (1884): "à M. Théodore Gad son ami / P Gauguin 84".

How Paul Gauguin signed

  • He signed his 1889 Self-Portrait "P Go" under the date; the same short form, written "PGo" or "PGO", appears on other works such as the drawing Nave Nave Fenua (probably 1894).
  • Gauguin inscribed Tahitian titles on most of the 66 paintings he took back to Paris in 1892; on Parau na te Varua ino he signed, dated and inscribed the title in the lower left corner.
  • In the Noa Noa woodcuts of 1893–94 he cut the monogram "PGO" into the block beside the Tahitian title.
  • Impressions of the Noa Noa woodcuts printed in 1921, after his death, carry the monogram cut in the block and a pencil note "Paul Gauguin fait" below the image rather than a signature in his hand.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Paul Gauguin sign paintings?

Gauguin often wrote a dedication above his signature on works given to friends, as on Still Life with Peonies (1884): "à M. Théodore Gad son ami / P Gauguin 84".

Did Paul Gauguin always sign the same way?

No. The 15 examples on this page, from 1884–1903, show abbreviation and date, full name and date, full name and date with Tahitian title, full name with title, drawn on the stone. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Paul Gauguin?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Paul Gauguin is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
