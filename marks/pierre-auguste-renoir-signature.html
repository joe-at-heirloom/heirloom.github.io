Pierre-Auguste Renoir's signature, 1864 to 1906

15 close-ups of Pierre-Auguste Renoir's signature (French, 1841–1919), from works dated 1864–1906, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

In the mid-1860s Renoir signed in capitals with his initial and the full year, as on Mademoiselle Sicot: 'A. RENOIR / 1865'.

How Pierre-Auguste Renoir signed

  • Small oil studies of 1866 carry only the initials 'AR'.
  • By the mid-1870s he usually dropped the initial and signed 'Renoir.' with a two-digit year, as on A Girl with a Watering Can: 'Renoir. 76.'
  • Some works left unsigned carry a stamped 'Renoir' atelier signature instead, as on Peaches on a Plate (1902/1905).

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Pierre-Auguste Renoir sign paintings?

In the mid-1860s Renoir signed in capitals with his initial and the full year, as on Mademoiselle Sicot: 'A. RENOIR / 1865'.

Did Pierre-Auguste Renoir always sign the same way?

No. The 15 examples on this page, from 1864–1906, show atelier stamp, initial, surname and date, initials, surname. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Pierre-Auguste Renoir?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Pierre-Auguste Renoir is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
