Albert Bierstadt's signature, 1862 to 1888

9 close-ups of Albert Bierstadt's signature (American (born in Germany), 1830–1902), from works dated 1862–1888, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Bierstadt's usual signature joins his initials into a monogram, so that 'AB' runs straight into the surname: museums record it as 'ABierstadt' with the AB in monogram.

How Albert Bierstadt signed

  • He often added a two-digit year under or after the signature, as in 'ABierstadt. / 66' on Mount Starr King, Yosemite (1866) and 'ABierstadt./64.' on Valley of the Yosemite.
  • His earliest painting at the National Gallery of Art, Lake Lucerne (1858), is already signed 'ABierstadt. / 1858.' at lower right.
  • The Last of the Buffalo (1888–89) is signed in full, 'Albert Bierstadt'; a photogravure of it was copyrighted by Bierstadt in 1891, and the National Gallery's impression is inscribed in pencil 'Senator Sherman / with Compliments of / Albert Bierstadt'.
  • Catalogues record the monogrammed signature at lower left on some works and lower right on others; on Mt. Baker, Washington (1890) it is at lower left.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Albert Bierstadt sign paintings?

Bierstadt's usual signature joins his initials into a monogram, so that 'AB' runs straight into the surname: museums record it as 'ABierstadt' with the AB in monogram.

Did Albert Bierstadt always sign the same way?

No. The 9 examples on this page, from 1862–1888, show full name, monogram signature, monogram signature and short date. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Albert Bierstadt?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Albert Bierstadt is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
