Pocket watch serial number lookup

Choose the maker named on the movement, enter its serial number, and see the year that serial was made, from the same lists Heirloom Advisor dates watches with.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

An American pocket watch is dated by the serial number engraved on its movement, not the case. Each maker numbered movements in sequence, so the serial falls between two year markers in that maker's list. Treat the year as approximate: movements were made in blocks and finished as needed.

Look up a movement serial

Use the number on the movement, not inside the case.

Read these before you rely on a year

  • Use the serial number engraved on the MOVEMENT (the works). Numbers stamped inside the case are the case maker's numbers and cannot be dated with this table.
  • How to read: pick the row for the maker with the largest serial_start that is <= the serial (and <= serial_end when present). 'year' is the primary list (Elite Timepieces, with the typo fixes named in the row note). 'alt_year', when present, is a different year that the cross-check source gives for those same serials; report such results as a range (e.g. 'c. 1903, possibly 1904').
  • Rows with boundary 'cross_check' are not year starts: they split a primary year where the cross-check list begins a new year, so alt_year covers only the serials it applies to. Only rows with boundary 'primary' are the standard 'first serial of the year' entries.
  • For coarse cross-check lists (PM Time often jumps 2-5 years between entries), a serial between two entries is read as 'somewhere in that span'; alt_year is only set when the primary year falls outside that span, and it is then the nearest year inside the span. The note on each such row gives the two bracketing entries.
  • All year lists are approximate. Serial numbers were allotted to production runs in blocks; plates could be blanked, stored and finished later as demand required, so a movement can have been finished a year or more after its nominal year, and runs were not always numbered in time order. The NAWCC encyclopedia says such lists are accurate to a year or two at best, with exceptions of 3 years or more. Say 'about' or 'circa' when citing a year.
  • The 'year start' figures are markers, not literal first serials of 1 January. Pocket Watch Site gives run dates for many Elgin entries (e.g. 25,001 on 20 Nov 1868; 10,000,000 on 15 May 1903), and the NAWCC Waltham ledger shows the Waltham year-start serials were delivered anywhere from January to December of the stated year.
  • Most free online lists (Elite Timepieces, PocketWatchRepair, Global Gemology, Pocket Watch Site, and Oldwatch for Waltham) are copies of the same standard list, so their agreement confirms the transcription, not the dates. Independent checks came from different compilations (PM Time, Oldwatch, Antique-Pocket-Watch/TheWatchGuy) and, for Waltham 1852-1891, the NAWCC ledger database.

Hamilton

Hamilton: table covers plain numeric serials 1 (1893) to 2,655,300 (1936) only. The standard list's 1937-1942 entries (2,900,000-4,500,000) were dropped because Oldwatch says 2,655,301-2,900,000 and 3,460,901-4,000,000 were never used and 3.1M-3.46M and 4.0M-4.52M were made over spans of 1928-1947; PocketWatchRepair puts the 987E (4,025,301-4,523,000) in 1928-1937. Letter-prefixed serials (C, S, 2B, 4C, G, J, etc.) and Hamilton wristwatch movements are not covered.

Hamilton lists disagree more than any other maker: Oldwatch dates most serials from about 780,000 to 2,655,300 three to five years earlier than the primary list (e.g. 1,000,000 is 1913 here, 1908 on Oldwatch; 2,440,000 is 1932 here, 1928 on Oldwatch), and PM Time's coarse list differs again (661,000 = 1910; 1,400,000 = 1920). Treat Hamilton results between 1905 and 1936 as uncertain by several years.

Illinois

Illinois (Illinois Springfield Watch Co., Illinois Watch Co., Springfield IL): table covers 5,000 (1872) to 5,600,000 (1948). Oldwatch says numbers before about 1880 were not made in serial order, so early Illinois dates are weak. PocketWatchRepair, Global Gemology and TheWatchGuy carry a variant for 1927-1931 (noted in those rows). A third list (PM Time, ccwatchmaker) places serials 2-7 years earlier between 1895 and 1915 (e.g. 2,460,000 = 1905 there, 1912 here); it disagrees with every other list and was used as the cross-check only below serial 162,001. Movements Illinois made for Burlington, Santa Fe Special, Washington Watch Co. (Montgomery Ward) and Plymouth (Sears) carry Illinois serials.

Hampden

Hampden (Hampden Watch Co., Springfield MA; Dueber-Hampden, Canton OH from 1888): weakest table. Primary list covers 59,000 (1877) to 3,980,000 (1927). Only 1897-1910 is well supported (primary and Oldwatch agree within a few thousand serials). Before 1897 Oldwatch differs by up to 3 years; after 1910 it differs by up to 7 years and runs to 4,600,000 by 1924, while the primary list stops at 3,980,000 (PocketWatchRepair itself states total output of about 4.6 million). PM Time is a third list that puts 1,000,000 in 1892 instead of 1897. Serials above 3,980,000 are past the primary list's last entry (read as 1927 or later), while Oldwatch dates 4,072,000-4,600,000 to 1921-1924; those rows carry that alt_year.

Questions

Where is the serial number on a pocket watch?

On the movement, the works inside, usually engraved on one of the plates or bridges. Open the back of the case carefully. A number stamped inside the case itself is the case maker's number and can't be dated with these tables.

How accurate is a pocket watch serial number date?

Close, not exact. Serials were allotted to production runs in blocks, and a movement could be finished a year or more after its nominal year. Where the published lists disagree, the lookup gives both years.

Does a serial number tell me a pocket watch's value?

No. It dates the movement; the grade, the jewel count, the case and its condition matter more, and many pocket watches have modest market value. A record is complete without a price.
