Gorham silver marks and date symbols

Gorham's lion, anchor and G marks its sterling from 1868, and a letter or symbol below it gives the year. Here is how to read both, with real marks from museum silver.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Gorham sterling is marked with a lion, an anchor and a Gothic G, usually with STERLING and a model number. Below them, a capital letter gives the year from 1868 (A) to 1884 (Q); from 1885 a small picture symbol does, such as a trident for 1897. Martelé, Gorham's hand-hammered line, is marked 950-1000 FINE or .9584.

Gorham, in brief

Jabez Gorham, a Providence jeweler, began making silver spoons in 1831, first as Gorham & Webster. The firm became the Gorham Manufacturing Company in the 1860s, and later the largest silver maker in the US. Before 1868 it worked in coin silver and marked its wares with the firm's name in a rectangle: Gorham & Webster, Gorham & Thurber, Gorham & Co. In 1868 it adopted the sterling standard, 925 thousandths, and the mark it has used since.

The lion, anchor and G

Gorham sterling carries three small marks in a row: a walking lion, an anchor and a Gothic capital G, usually with STERLING and a model number below. They look like British hallmarks, and are often taken for them, but they are Gorham's trademark: the US had no official assay marks, and the lion and anchor (Birmingham's town mark) were borrowed for the look. The G is for Gorham.

Gorham also made electroplate. Museum records of its plated ware show an anchor alone with GORHAM CO., or GORHAM with E.P., rather than the lion, anchor and G with STERLING. Read the lion and G with the word STERLING as sterling, and an anchor with the name but no STERLING as a reason to check for plate.

Gorham date marks

From 1868 Gorham struck a yearly date mark on hollowware and some small wares, on its own line below STERLING and the model number. Most flatware has none. Two systems were used:

  • 1868 to 1884: a capital letter, A for 1868 through Q for 1884.
  • From 1885: a small picture symbol for each year, such as a trident or a key.

Each year from 1885 had its own small symbol. The full chart is printed in Carpenter's Gorham Silver and Rainwater's Encyclopedia; it could not be checked against a second source year by year, so it is not reproduced here. These are the years museum records tie to a symbol:

Later Gorham silver, from 1941, has been said to carry a shape whose number of sides gives the decade; only one museum example (a hexagon on a pitcher of 1960) was found for it.

Martelé

Martelé (French for hammered) was Gorham's hand-raised, hand-hammered Art Nouveau line, designed under William C. Codman and shown at the Paris Exposition of 1900. It was made in silver finer than sterling, and marked differently: the word Martelé in script, a spread eagle, the lion, anchor and G, then 950-1000 FINE or .9584, with a model number and often a letter code. Some carry a retailer's name, such as Spaulding & Co. of Chicago.

A thesis on the line gives 950 from 1898 and .9584 (the old British Britannia standard) from 1905; both marks are confirmed on museum silver, but those years rest on that one study. No source found here explains the letter codes, so they are not decoded here.

Read these before you rely on a mark

  • The lion, anchor and G is a company mark, not a government hallmark: it says Gorham made and sold the item as sterling.
  • A date mark dates the making of the item, not its engraving or later repair.
  • Many Gorham symbols after 1885 are small and alike. Where this page gives only one museum for a year, check a full chart before you rely on it.

Questions

What is the Gorham silver mark?

A walking lion, an anchor and a Gothic G in a row, usually with STERLING and a model number. Gorham has used it on sterling since 1868. It imitates British hallmarks but is Gorham's own trademark.

How do I read Gorham date marks?

Look below STERLING and the model number for a single letter or a small symbol. A capital letter from A to Q is a year from 1868 (A) to 1884 (Q). From 1885 each year has a picture symbol, such as a trident for 1897.

Is all Gorham silver sterling?

No. Gorham made electroplate too, marked with an anchor and the name, or with E.P., and no STERLING. Before 1868 it made coin silver, marked with the firm's name.

What does 950-1000 FINE mean on Gorham?

It marks Gorham's Martelé line, made in silver of 950 thousandths, finer than sterling. Later Martelé is marked .9584.
