Royal Doulton marks

Doulton's marks changed with the firm: stoneware stamps at Lambeth, a crowned mark at Burslem, then the lion standing on a crown from the royal warrant of 1901. Small additions to that mark narrow the date.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Doulton began making stoneware at Lambeth, London, in the early 1800s and added a works at Burslem in Staffordshire in the 1870s 1, 2, 9. The lion on a crown with Royal Doulton dates from 1901 or 1902 3, 5; Made in England was added about 1930 4, 7; and a small number beside the lion, when there is one, gives the year: add 1927 5, 7.

Who made Royal Doulton

John Doulton went into partnership with John Watts at Lambeth, making salt-glazed stoneware bottles, jugs and later drainpipes, as Doulton & Watts 1, 2, 3. Watts died in 1858 1, 2. From the late 1860s students of the Lambeth School of Art, among them George Tinworth and Hannah Barlow, decorated stoneware there, and the art wares made Doulton's name 1, 2, 9.

In 1877 Doulton went into partnership with Pinder, Bourne & Co. of the Nile Street works at Burslem, which became Doulton & Co. in 1882 and added porcelain soon after 1, 2, 9.

In 1901 Edward VII granted the firm a royal warrant and the right to call its wares Royal Doulton 5, 9. Production at Lambeth stopped in 1956 4, 6, 9. The BBC reported that the last Royal Doulton factory, at Nile Street, closed in April 2005, with most making moved overseas and some limited work kept in Stoke 11.

Royal Doulton marks, in order

Doulton & Watts, Lambeth

To 1858

Doulton & Watts with Lambeth Pottery and London, impressed in brown salt-glazed stoneware 1, 3.

Doulton Lambeth, impressed

About 1858 to 1956

Doulton Lambeth in an oval, a circle or a rosette, impressed in the stoneware, later with England 2, 3. In the 1870s and 1880s the year is often impressed in the middle of the mark, which dates the item directly 3, 9, 10. Special bodies had marks of their own: Lambeth Faience, Impasto, Silicon, Carrara, Marqueterie and Crown Lambeth 1, 2, 3.

Artists' monograms

The 1870s on

The Lambeth artists incised their own initials or monograms beside the factory mark, such as HBB for Hannah Barlow and GT for George Tinworth 1, 3, 10.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

The Doulton Burslem crown

1882 to about 1902

A crown over a rosette reading Doulton Burslem, with interlaced Ds in the middle, printed on Burslem earthenware and china 1, 2, 3. Cushion dates it to about 1912; most sources end it with the lion mark of 1902 3, 5.

The lion and crown, Royal Doulton

From 1901 or 1902

A lion standing on a crown over a circle of four interlaced Ds, with Royal Doulton around it, printed on Burslem wares; Lambeth stoneware has impressed Royal Doulton versions 3, 5, 9. It marks the royal warrant of 1901, so the lion mark alone means 1901 or later 5, 9.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Made in England added

From about 1930

Made in England was added above the lion and crown from about 1930 4, 7. A mark without it is most likely earlier, though one Charlton catalog puts the change at about 1920 (see below).

The year number beside the lion

1928 to 1954

A small number printed just to the right of the lion and crown gives the year: 1 for 1928, so add 1927 to the number, up to 27 for 1954 5, 7. It is often missing 5, 7.

HN numbers on figures

From 1913

Royal Doulton figures carry an HN number, starting with HN1 in 1913. HN is the initials of Harry Nixon, who ran the figure painters 5, 8, 9. The number names the model, not the year it was made; many models stayed in production for decades 5, 9.

No photograph of this mark under an open license was found, so it is described in words only.

Copies and later marks to be careful with

  • The Antiques Trade Gazette reported fake Royal Doulton bulldogs carrying the lion and crown mark in 2000, and in 2002 a Trading Standards warning about fake Lambeth wares made from molds taken off genuine items, with numbers on the base in felt-tip pen 12.
  • The BBC reported in 2004 that Royal Doulton's making was moving overseas, where much of it had already gone 11. A modern Royal Doulton mark may name another country.
  • An HN number or a model number dates the design, not the item. Check the backstamp around it 5, 9.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • When Doulton began at Lambeth: 1815, in the V&A's records 9; 1818, in Jervis's 1902 encyclopedia, though his entry for Watts gives 1815 2.
  • The lion and crown: from 1902 according to Cushion 3; introduced in 1901 with the warrant according to Eyles and Dennis 5.
  • Made in England: about 1930 according to Godden and Dale's catalog of jugs 4, 7; about 1920 according to the Charlton catalog of figurines (1994) 7.

Questions

How do I date Royal Doulton by the backstamp?

The lion and crown means 1901 or later; with Made in England, about 1930 or later; and a small number to the right of the lion, from 1928 to 1954, gives the year when you add 1927 3, 4, 5, 7.

What does the number next to the Royal Doulton lion mean?

It is a year code: 1 is 1928, 10 is 1937, up to 27 for 1954. Many items have none 5, 7.

What does HN mean on a Royal Doulton figure?

Harry Nixon, who ran the figure painters when the series began in 1913. The HN number names the model, not the year 5, 8, 9.

Is Doulton Lambeth the same as Royal Doulton?

It is the same firm's stoneware works in London, which marked its ware Doulton Lambeth and later Royal Doulton until it closed in 1956 3, 4, 9.

When did Royal Doulton stop making pottery in England?

The last Royal Doulton factory in England, at Nile Street in Burslem, closed in April 2005, the BBC reported 11.
