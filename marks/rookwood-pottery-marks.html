Rookwood pottery marks and dates

Rookwood's RP monogram counts the years in flames, then in Roman numerals. Count yours to get the year.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Rookwood dated its pottery with the flames around the RP monogram in the base: no flames is 1886, one flame was added each year to 14 in 1900, and after that a Roman numeral below the flames counts from 1900 (XXII is 1922). Historic production ended in 1967.

Date a Rookwood mark

Count only the short flames around the RP monogram, not the tail of the R. Leave the numeral empty if there is none.

What the mark looks like

The marks at each stage: the name and year to 1886, the bare RP monogram, one flame, the full ring of fourteen, and the numerals that follow. The 1930 photograph shows XXX under the flames; the shape number below it (not shown) is not a date.

How Rookwood dated its marks

1887–1900RP monogram with 1 to 14 flames and no numeral belowAdd one year per flame.
1901–1967RP monogram with 14 flames and an impressed Roman numeral below (I to LXVII)Add the numeral to 1900.

Read these before you rely on a year

  • A date code only means something next to the right factory mark (impressed WEDGWOOD, impressed MINTON(S), the Royal Worcester printed mark, the Rookwood RP monogram, the Sevres interlaced Ls). A code alone does not identify the maker, and several of these systems were copied or forged (Minton by smaller makers; Sevres very widely).
  • The codes give a single year only when read exactly. Letters that look alike (I/J, U/V, O/Q, C/G) and blurred or glaze-filled impressions are the main source of error. When a reading is uncertain, report the candidate years.
  • Count only the flames (short rays) around the RP monogram. The tail of the reversed R looks like a flame and must not be counted (K2). Small impressed finishers' dots near the flames are not flames or dates (K1, K4).
  • The Roman numeral sits below the 14-flame RP mark. Directly below it is an Arabic shape number, often with a size letter A-F (e.g. 907C) (K1, K2, K4). Do not read the shape number as a year.
  • Letters after the shape number are not dates: V = vellum glaze (from 1904), Z = mat-glaze line (c.1900-1904), Y = architectural faience (K1).
  • A hand-scored or wheel-cut X on the base marks a second, or a item withdrawn from sale; with a third stroke it marks a giveaway. It is not the numeral X (1910). The date numeral is impressed in line below the flame mark (K1).
  • Kiln-shaped anniversary marks containing 50 (1930 firing), 61 (1940-1941), 70 (1950) or 75 (1955) are anniversary numbers, not the date (K1). Some 1930-1931 items also carry a semicircle of hash marks (K1).
  • Before 1882 there was no consistent mark: painted or incised names, an anchor, or an oval 'Rookwood Pottery, 207 Eastern Avenue, 1882, Cincinnati' (K2). 1882-1886: ROOKWOOD in block capitals with the year in Arabic numerals (K1, K2).
  • Production ended in 1967, so historic Roman numerals run from I (1901) to LXVII (1967) (K3, K4). 1960-1967 items made in Mississippi also carry 'Rookwood Pottery Starkville Miss' (K2, K5).
  • Revival marks: under Arthur Townley (from 1982/83 to 2005) the flame mark was used with Arabic-numeral dates (K2). The modern Cincinnati company uses Roman numerals with an MM prefix, e.g. MMXII = 2012 (K2, K5). So XII = 1912 but MMXII = 2012. Never add 1900 to a numeral that begins with MM.
  • Sources differ only in wording on the start. K2 and K5 say 1886 has no flames and one flame was added from 1887 (1 flame = 1887). K1, K3 and K4 say a flame was added each year 1886-1900 with 14 in 1900, and K4's example (8 flames = 1894) uses the same count. All agree 14 flames = 1900, and year = 1886 + flames fits every source.

Questions

How do I date Rookwood pottery?

Find the RP monogram impressed in the base and count the flames around it: no flames is 1886, and each flame adds a year to 14 flames in 1900. After that, a Roman numeral below the 14 flames counts the years from 1900, so XIV is 1914.

What are the other numbers and letters on the base?

The Arabic number below the numeral is a shape number, often with a size letter (907C). Letters such as V or Z after it name a glaze line. Neither is a date.

My numeral begins with MM. Is that a Rookwood date?

Yes, but a modern one: the revived company writes the full year in Roman numerals, so MMXII is 2012, not 1912.
