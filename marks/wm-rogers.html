Wm. Rogers marks, and the other Rogers names

Half a dozen firms stamped Rogers on silver plate. Here is which one made yours, and what the stars, anchors and eagles in the mark tell you.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Wm. Rogers marks are on silver-plated flatware from two Connecticut makers that used William Rogers's name: Wm. Rogers Mfg. Co. of Hartford (Wm. Rogers & Son, from 1865) and Simpson, Hall, Miller & Co. of Wallingford (Wm. Rogers between an eagle and a star, from 1878). Both became part of International Silver. Wm. A. Rogers is a different, unrelated firm.

Who made it

William Rogers (1801–1873) was a Hartford, Connecticut, silversmith and one of the three Rogers brothers behind 1847 Rogers Bros. After the brothers' Hartford works closed in 1862, his name went on to two more makers of plated flatware.

  • Wm. Rogers Mfg. Co., Hartford: formed in 1865 by partners who agreed with William Rogers and his son to use their name, and incorporated in 1872. It used WM. ROGERS & SON and 1865 WM. ROGERS MFG. CO. It joined the International Silver Co. in 1898 and its work was moved to Meriden after 1903.
  • Simpson, Hall, Miller & Co., Wallingford, Connecticut: from 1878 it stamped WM. ROGERS between an eagle and a star under a contract with William Rogers Jr. Wm. Rogers Mfg. Co. sued, and in 1887 the Connecticut Supreme Court let the Wallingford firm keep using the name. It joined International Silver, which a 1906 history dates to 1899 and a later reference book to 1898.

International Silver went on using both marks, the eagle-and-star mark on hollowware from 1919. The trademark registrations later passed to Syratech, not to Oneida.

Which Rogers is yours?

Rogers is the most crowded name in American plate. The brothers licensed it, other Rogers families used it in their own right, and the courts of the 1880s and 1890s stopped several firms from borrowing it. Read the whole mark, including stars, anchors, eagles and initials:

We haven't yet found an openly licensed photograph of a Wm. Rogers mark clear enough to show; the marks are stamped on the back of the handle.

Is it plate or sterling?

Plate. Every Wm. Rogers mark was registered and listed for silver-plated ware, and we found no sterling made under the name. Simpson, Hall, Miller did make sterling from 1895, but under its own marks.

Read the whole stamp first. American sterling is marked STERLING. Plate is marked with the maker's name and often a grade word or number, such as A1, XII, XIV, TRIPLE or QUADRUPLE PLATE, and a grade mark appears only on plate.

Then look at the places that wear. Plate is a thin coat of silver over a base metal, usually nickel silver, so on a well-used spoon the tip of the bowl, the back of the bowl and the ends of fork tines turn yellowish or gray where the silver has worn through to the metal underneath.

How to date Wm. Rogers

American plate makers didn't stamp a year. A number in the mark, such as 1847 or 1865, is the year a firm said it was founded and stayed on the ware for decades. To date an item, name its pattern (the design of the handle) and find when that pattern was introduced. A patent date stamped on a handle (PAT. followed by a date) is the earliest the design can be, not the year the item was made, and makers kept producing a popular pattern for decades.

  • An eagle and star around WM. ROGERS: 1878 or later.
  • ROGERS between two anchors: 1887 or later.
  • WM. A. ROGERS, in any form: 1895 or later, and a different firm.
  • 1865 in the mark is the firm's founding year, like 1847 on 1847 Rogers Bros.

Questions

Is Wm. Rogers the same as Wm. A. Rogers?

No. Wm. Rogers marks come from William Rogers's Hartford firm and the Wallingford firm he and his son licensed, both later International Silver. Wm. A. Rogers was a separate New York business whose marks begin in 1895–96 and later belonged to Oneida.

Is Wm. Rogers the same as 1847 Rogers Bros.?

Related, not the same. William Rogers was one of the 1847 brothers, but Wm. Rogers marks are from firms formed after 1865 that used his name. Both lines ended up with International Silver.

Is Wm. Rogers silverware real silver?

It is silver plate, a coat of silver over a base metal. We found no sterling made under the Wm. Rogers name.

What do the star and eagle in the mark mean?

They are the brand of Simpson, Hall, Miller & Co. of Wallingford, Connecticut, used from 1878 under a contract with William Rogers Jr. An item with them was made in 1878 or later.

Does 1865 in the mark mean it was made in 1865?

No. 1865 is the year Wm. Rogers Mfg. Co. was formed, and it stayed in the mark for years.
