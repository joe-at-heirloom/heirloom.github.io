Chenghua mark 成化

The Chenghua reign of the Ming dynasty ran 1465–1487 (some tables to 1488). Its mark reads 大明成化年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Imperial Chenghua wares carry the six-character regular-script mark. Genuine Chenghua porcelain is scarce. Regular script only, never seal; the double square frame first appears in this reign. Small doucai jars of the reign are marked with the single character 天 in underglaze blue (the 'tian jars'), a form later copied under Qianlong.1 The most frequently copied mark of all (with Xuande). Used on 16th-century wares and, most of all, on Kangxi-period porcelain: Hobson says a fine Chenghua-marked item can generally be assumed to be Kangxi; Gulland's editor notes the same. Also on many modern inferior wares.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

The Chenghua mark

  • 大明成化年製 · 6 characters, regular script. Standard imperial form in underglaze blue; within a double circle or, on small cups, a double square.
  • 天 · 1 characters, regular script. A single 天 in underglaze blue on the base of small doucai jars of the reign; copied under Qianlong.
  • 成化年製 · 4 characters, regular script. Not established as a mark of the period: four-character Chenghua marks are generally later (one was used on Kangxi wares).
  • 成化年製 · 4 characters, seal script. Bushell: the stamped seal form on 'old crackle' vases is fictitious.
1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9

Years: 1465–1487 (some tables to 1488). 1465-1487 standard (era ran to 13 January 1488). UCSD and Gulland give 1465-1488.2, 3, 4, 10, 11

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Ch'eng-hua, Ch'eng Hua, Ching-hwa (Gulland), Tching-hoa (Bushell, after French writers).

On imperial wares

Later items with this mark

The most frequently copied mark of all (with Xuande). Used on 16th-century wares and, most of all, on Kangxi-period porcelain: Hobson says a fine Chenghua-marked item can generally be assumed to be Kangxi; Gulland's editor notes the same. Also on many modern inferior wares.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
