McCoy pottery marks

Three different businesses in Roseville, Ohio, carried the McCoy name, and their marks are easy to mix up. The word McCoy on an item points to one of them, or to a later firm that took the name.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

J. W. McCoy's pottery (1899 to 1911) became Brush-McCoy, then Brush; it rarely marked its ware McCoy 1, 2, 3. The Nelson McCoy pottery (1910 to 1990) used an NM monogram in the 1930s and the name McCoy from about 1940 1, 2, 3. Since 1991 others have registered the McCoy name for new pottery 9, 10.

Who made McCoy pottery

James W. McCoy started the J. W. McCoy Pottery in Roseville, Ohio, in 1899, making art ware and everyday ware 1, 2. In 1911 it merged with George Brush's pottery as the Brush-McCoy Pottery Company, which dropped McCoy from its name in 1925 and closed as the Brush Pottery in 1982 1, 2, 4.

In 1910 J. W. McCoy's son Nelson started the Nelson McCoy Sanitary Stoneware Company in Roseville, renamed the Nelson McCoy Pottery Company in 1933 1, 2. After Nelson McCoy died in 1945 his nephew Nelson McCoy Melick ran it, then Nelson McCoy Jr. from 1954 1, 2, 5.

The pottery came under the owners of the Mount Clemens Pottery in 1967, the Chase group in 1972 and Lancaster Colony in 1974 1, 2, 5, 6. Designer Accents bought it in July 1986 2, 7, and it closed in December 1990 2, 8.

McCoy marks, in order

Many McCoy items of every period carry only a shape number, U.S.A., or nothing at all 1, 3.

J. W. McCoy line names, impressed

About 1899 to 1911

The early ware is often impressed with a line name and a number rather than the McCoy name: Rosewood, Mt. Pelee, Loy-Nel-Art; the Kovels record Loy-Nel-Art with McCoy from about 1906 1, 2, 3. Much J. W. McCoy ware is unmarked 1, 3.

Brush-McCoy and Brush

1911 to 1925, then to 1982

Brush-McCoy used an ink stamp with the company's name, the line name and Zanesville, O., or only a shape number or a paper label 2, 3. After 1925 the ware is marked Brush, with a number and USA 1, 3. Neither is Nelson McCoy.

The NM monogram

About 1934 to 1940

An NM monogram with U.S.A., impressed, used by the Nelson McCoy pottery in its early years of decorative ware 2, 3.

The McCoy name

From about 1940

McCoy in its own lettering, alone or with U.S.A. or Made in U.S.A., mostly raised from the mold, some impressed 2, 3. Designer Accents later swore that the name was first used as a mark at the end of 1940 9. A silver foil label reading McCoy Hand Painted was also used 2, 3.

Floraline

Disputed

Floraline with U.S.A. and a number marks the pottery's line for florists 1, 3. The Kovels date a Floraline mark to about 1935; collectors usually give 1960 2.

LCC with McCoy

About 1974 to 1986

A stylized LCC, for Lancaster Colony Corporation, impressed beside McCoy and U.S.A., sometimes with a year 1, 2, 3.

Later marks and copies to be careful with

  • In 1992 a Tennessee man, Roger Jensen, applied to register McCoy as a trademark for pottery, claiming use from 1991; it was registered in 1993 and cancelled in 1999 after Designer Accents petitioned against it 9, 10. The collectibles columnist Harry Rinker reported that he made new pottery marked McCoy, including a Little Red Riding Hood cookie jar, a design McCoy never made 11.
  • The trademark office records the McCoy name as registered since 2005 to an unrelated Georgia firm for new earthenware, including cookie jars and planters 9. New items marked McCoy are legal and are not from the old pottery.
  • Brush and Brush-McCoy ware is often sold as McCoy. The marks say Brush, or carry only a number 1, 3.
  • Other potteries in Roseville, Ohio, such as Robinson-Ransbottom, put the town's name in their marks; Roseville in a mark is not McCoy 1, 2.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • Designer Accents' purchase: July 18, 1986, in a 1987 trade journal and the Kovels 2, 7; collectors often give 1985.
  • Floraline: about 1935 in the Kovels' dictionary 2, 1960 in collectors' accounts.

Questions

How do I tell which McCoy made my pottery?

J. W. McCoy ware (1899 to 1911) usually has a line name or nothing; Brush-McCoy and Brush say Brush or carry a number; the Nelson McCoy pottery used NM in the 1930s and McCoy from about 1940 1, 2, 3.

What does LCC on McCoy mean?

Lancaster Colony Corporation, which owned the Nelson McCoy pottery from 1974 to 1986 1, 2, 7.

When did McCoy close?

The Nelson McCoy pottery closed in December 1990 2, 8.

Is new pottery marked McCoy fake?

Not necessarily. The McCoy name has been registered by other firms since the 1990s, and new pottery has been made under it legally 9, 10. Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine.
