Edgar Degas's signature, 1855 to 1899

12 close-ups of Edgar Degas's signature (French, 1834–1917), from works dated 1855–1899, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

An early drawing of his brother is inscribed and signed with his initial: 'à René / E Degas 1855.'

How Edgar Degas signed

  • Around 1871 he still sometimes signed 'E. Degas' with the year; most later works are signed simply 'Degas'.
  • Works left in his studio at his death were stamped with a printed 'Degas' signature, usually red, cataloged by Lugt as no. 658 and described by the National Gallery of Art as the vente (sale) stamp.
  • A second studio stamp, an oval reading 'ATELIER ED. DEGAS' (Lugt 657), was also applied to works from the studio, often on the back.
  • The signature stamp was not always red: on the monotype The Green Room it is blue-gray.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Edgar Degas sign paintings?

An early drawing of his brother is inscribed and signed with his initial: 'à René / E Degas 1855.'

Did Edgar Degas always sign the same way?

No. The 12 examples on this page, from 1855–1899, show atelier stamp (Lugt 657), initial, surname and date, inscribed, initial, surname and date, studio stamp (Lugt 658). Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Edgar Degas?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Edgar Degas is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
