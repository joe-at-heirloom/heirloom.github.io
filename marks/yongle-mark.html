Yongle mark 永樂

The Yongle reign of the Ming dynasty ran 1403–1424 (some tables from 1402). Its mark reads 永樂年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Genuine Yongle marks are four characters, 永樂年製, in archaic seal script, incised or impressed, and rare; a Shanghai Museum text says they are seen only on stem cups. Most Yongle official wares are unmarked.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Hobson (1915) notes not-uncommon blue-and-white cups with a four-character Yongle mark that are 'hardly older than the last dynasty' (Qing).2, 5

The Yongle mark

  • 永樂年製 · 4 characters, seal script. Archaic seal script, incised or faintly etched (sometimes under the glaze), notably inside white eggshell 'bodiless' bowls and stem cups.
  • 永樂年製 · 4 characters, regular script. Bushell records a regular-script form too; Hobson calls regular-script four-character Yongle cups he saw later than Ming.
1, 2, 3, 5, 6

Years: 1403–1424 (some tables from 1402). 1403-1424 in most tables; UCSD gives 1402 (the year he seized the throne). The era's last Chinese year ended 19 January 1425.1, 6, 7, 8, 9

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Yung-lo, Yung Lo, Young-lo.

On imperial wares

Later items with this mark

Hobson (1915) notes not-uncommon blue-and-white cups with a four-character Yongle mark that are 'hardly older than the last dynasty' (Qing).2, 5

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
