Zhengde mark 正德

The Zhengde reign of the Ming dynasty ran 1506–1521 (some tables from 1505). Its mark reads 大明正德年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Six-character and four-character regular-script marks; Bushell calls the mark comparatively rare, found on green-dragon bowls and blue-and-white, including items with Arabic/Persian inscriptions.1 Later copies with Zhengde marks exist (仿正德款), both close copies and later-style items with only the mark borrowed. Zhengde official wares themselves sometimes carry Xuande marks.1, 2

The Zhengde mark

  • 大明正德年製 · 6 characters, regular script. Underglaze blue.
  • 正德年製 · 4 characters, regular script. Also used on official wares.
  • Rare four-character Zhengde marks in Phags-pa script are recorded on official wares.
1, 2, 3

Years: 1506–1521 (some tables from 1505). 1506-1521 standard; UCSD gives 1505 (accession).3, 4, 5, 6, 7

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Cheng-te, Cheng Te, Ching-tih (Gulland).

On imperial wares

Later items with this mark

Later copies with Zhengde marks exist (仿正德款), both close copies and later-style items with only the mark borrowed. Zhengde official wares themselves sometimes carry Xuande marks.1, 2

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
