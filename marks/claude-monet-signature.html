Claude Monet's signature, 1864 to 1915

15 close-ups of Claude Monet's signature (French, 1840–1926), from works dated 1864–1915, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Monet signed with his full name, 'Claude Monet', from the 1860s to the 1900s, usually adding a two-digit year in the 1870s–1890s and the full year in the 1900s.

How Claude Monet signed

  • He did not always sign when he painted: on Boats on the Beach at Étretat (1885) the signature was added after the picture had already been varnished.
  • A signed date is not always the year of painting: The Artist's Garden at Vétheuil, painted in 1881, is signed 'Claude Monet 80'.
  • Many late canvases were never signed; when they left the studio after his death they were given a stamped 'Claude Monet' signature, which the Art Institute of Chicago notes does not quite match his own handwriting.
  • The estate stamps were probably applied by his son Michel, who inherited the studio, likely with the Paris dealer Katia Granoff, who sold many of the late works.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Claude Monet sign paintings?

Monet signed with his full name, 'Claude Monet', from the 1860s to the 1900s, usually adding a two-digit year in the 1870s–1890s and the full year in the 1900s.

Did Claude Monet always sign the same way?

No. The 15 examples on this page, from 1864–1915, show full name, full name and date, studio stamp (posthumous). Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Claude Monet?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Claude Monet is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
