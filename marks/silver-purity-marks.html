What 925, 800 and other numbers on silver mean

925, 800, 900, 958, 999: the number on silver is how much of it is silver. Here is each common standard and where it comes from.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

A number on silver is its fineness in parts per 1000. 925 is sterling (92.5% silver), 800 is 80%, 900 is American coin silver, 958 is Britannia silver and 999 is fine silver. In Britain an assay office strikes the number after testing; in the United States the maker stamps it, and the law holds the maker to it.

A number on silver is its fineness: the parts of pure silver in 1000 parts of the metal. The rest is other metal, which makes it harder: pure silver, and even the Britannia standard, is soft. The same reading works wherever the number comes from.

Common standards

This table lists the standards the sources on this page set down in law or record. Other numbers occur on silver from elsewhere; read them the same way, as parts in 1000, alongside the country's other marks.

Before the numbers: ounces and pennyweights

Older English law didn't write 925. It set the standard as 11 ounces 2 pennyweights of silver in every pound troy of 12 ounces, which works out to 925 in 1000. The Britannia standard of 1697 was 11 ounces 10 pennyweights, which is 958.3 in 1000; the Hallmarking Act of 1973 states it as 958.4.

How each country marks the number

  • United Kingdom. Today the compulsory hallmark is the sponsor's (maker's) mark, the fineness as a number in a shield, and the assay office's mark. The lion passant and Britannia are optional symbols beside the number, and the date letter has been optional since 1999. On older British silver the symbols alone carry the standard: a lion passant for sterling, Britannia for the higher standard.
  • United States. STERLING, or a number, stamped by the maker. Federal law requires 925 for STERLING and 900 for COIN, and a stated number must be met within four parts in 1000.
  • Germany, from 1888. The law of 1884 let makers state a fineness on silver wares only at 800 or more, with a crown and a crescent moon beside the number and the firm's mark. It allowed eight parts in 1000 below the stated number.

Questions

What does 925 mean on silver?

925 means 925 parts in 1000 of the metal are silver: 92.5%. That is sterling, the standard of the British lion passant and of STERLING on American silver.

What does 800 mean on silver?

800 means 80% silver. It is the lowest standard the UK hallmarks, and the lowest fineness German law let a maker state on silver wares from 1888, beside a crescent moon and a crown.

Is 800 silver real silver?

Yes: it is solid silver at a lower standard than sterling, 800 parts in 1000 rather than 925. It is not plate.

What is the difference between 925 and 999 silver?

999 is fine silver, 99.9% pure; 925 is sterling, 92.5% silver. Pure silver is soft, so most tableware and jewelry is made at a lower standard such as 925.

Does a 925 stamp prove an item is silver?

No. It is a statement of the standard, made by an assay office in Britain and by the maker in the United States. A test of the metal is the only proof.
