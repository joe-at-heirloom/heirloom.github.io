Sèvres date letters, 1753 to 1793

The letter inside Sèvres' interlaced Ls dates the painting of the mark. Type it to see the year in both published tables.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Vincennes and Sèvres porcelain carries a painted date letter inside or beside the interlaced Ls: A for 1753 or 1754, through Z for 1777, then doubled letters from AA in 1778 to the early 1790s. K to Z and AA to II mean the same year in both published tables; the others differ by one year.

Find a Sèvres date letter

Type the painted letter inside the interlaced Ls: one letter, or a doubled letter from 1778.

What the mark looks like

Two script Ls crossed back to back, painted, usually in blue. Before the letters began the Ls carried a dot or nothing; from 1753 or 1754 a letter inside them gives the year. Marks painted below the Ls are painters' and gilders' marks, not dates.

Every Sèvres date letter, 1753 to 1793

Two tables are published. The revised one (the French Porcelain Society, after David Peters, 2014) is used by museums today; the traditional one is a year earlier for A to I and a year later for KK to PP. Both are shown. Read these before you rely on a year

  • A date code only means something next to the right factory mark (impressed WEDGWOOD, impressed MINTON(S), the Royal Worcester printed mark, the Rookwood RP monogram, the Sevres interlaced Ls). A code alone does not identify the maker, and several of these systems were copied or forged (Minton by smaller makers; Sevres very widely).
  • The codes give a single year only when read exactly. Letters that look alike (I/J, U/V, O/Q, C/G) and blurred or glaze-filled impressions are the main source of error. When a reading is uncertain, report the candidate years.
  • There are two published date-letter tables. TRADITIONAL (S2, S5, and S3 for single letters): A = 1753, one letter a year, W never used, Z = 1777; doubles AA = 1778 to PP = 1793, which requires JJ = 1787. REVISED (S1, after David Peters 2014; used by the V&A, S4): A = 1754 to H = 1761, I and J are one letter (1762), and K to Z match the traditional table. Doubles AA to HH match; II/JJ = 1786; KK = 1787 to PP = 1792; QQ = 1793. S3 agrees with the revised doubles (AA to QQ, no JJ). In each row, 'year' is the revised (S1) value and 'alt_year' the traditional one, where they differ.
  • Practical effect: single letters K to Z (1763 to 1777) and doubles AA to II (1778 to 1786) are the same in both tables. For A to I and for JJ to PP, give the year as a two-year range (e.g. 'H: 1760 or 1761; 1761 in current scholarship').
  • Letters skipped: W is never used (S1, S3; the counts in S2 and S5 need it skipped). J: the traditional table uses I (1761) and J (1762) as separate letters; the revised table treats I and J as one letter (1762). JJ: the revised table (S1) treats II and JJ as one pair (1786), S3 says JJ is not used, and the traditional end point PP = 1793 (S2, S5) needs JJ = 1787.
  • The two letters of a double are always the same. A pair of different letters (e.g. 'CL', 'VX') is not a date code, and S3 says it indicates a fake mark. Letters may be upper or lower case, with no difference in meaning (S1).
  • Misreadings happen even in collections: the Bowes Museum re-read a cup's letters as dd (1781) where an old label had read jj (S6). Painters' and gilders' marks (dots, anchors, crescents, initials, numbers such as 2000) sit round the cipher and are not date letters (S1).
  • The letter is inside or beside the interlaced Ls, painted (usually blue enamel) rather than impressed. A crown above the Ls marks hard paste (from about 1769/1770 per S3; from the 1770s per S1). Soft paste has no crown (S1, S3).
  • The date letter was used until 1793. S3 says QQ was used until 17 July 1793. After that, marks are RF (Republique Francaise) and/or the word Sevres (S2, S3). S1 calls the Revolutionary marks of 1793-1800 complex and refers to Peters (2005/2015); this table does not cover them.
  • 19th- and 20th-century marks are separate systems, not covered here. Napoleon I: 'M. Nle Sevres', then 'M. Imple de Sevres' with a year sign. Restoration: crowned double L with fleur-de-lis (Louis XVIII), double C (Charles X). LP marks with the two-digit year (Louis-Philippe). From 1848: an 'S' with the two-digit year in a cartouche; under Napoleon III a crowned N with the year. 1928-1940: an S in a roundel with a lower-case letter (b = 1929, e = 1932, j = 1937). Georges Mathieu's mark from about 1970 (S1, S3). FPS example: a green printed 'S 62' lozenge = glazed and fired 1862, with a red crowned N over '63' for gilding in 1863 (S1).
  • Sevres marks are among the most forged in porcelain. S3 notes that a clumsy cipher containing an S is often a fake, because forgers read the S as 'Sevres', although S genuinely equals 1771. Treat any date letter as the date the mark claims, not as proof the item is genuine factory work (S3, S7).

Questions

How do I date Sèvres porcelain?

Find the painted interlaced Ls and the letter inside or beside them. Single letters run from A (1753 or 1754) to Z (1777), then doubled letters from AA (1778) to the early 1790s. The letter dates the painting of the mark, which is usually the decoration.

Does a Sèvres mark mean my porcelain is Sèvres?

No. The interlaced Ls are among the most copied marks in porcelain. The letter tells you the date the mark claims; whether the item is factory work is a question for a specialist.

What if the two letters are different?

The two letters of a double are always the same. Different letters, such as CL, are not a date code.
