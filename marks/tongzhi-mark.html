Tongzhi mark 同治

The Tongzhi reign of the Qing dynasty ran 1862–1874 (some tables to 1875). Its mark reads 大清同治年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The imperial kiln, idle from 1855, was rebuilt in 1866. Marked wares exist and are of modest quality by most accounts; regular script is usual, with seal script incised or gilt on teadust and flambé glazes. Wares made for the emperor's wedding (1872) carry the marks 燕喜同和 and 長春同慶 as well as the reign mark.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 No common later copies are recorded in the sources.6

The Tongzhi mark

  • 大清同治年製 · 6 characters, regular script. The usual imperial form: underglaze blue, two columns of three.
  • 大清同治年製 · 6 characters, seal script. Incised or gilt, on teadust and flambé glazes.
2, 6, 7, 8

Years: 1862–1874 (some tables to 1875). 1862-1874 standard (era ran into February 1875; emperor died January 1875). Gulland 1862-1875; Hobson vol. 2 has 1862-1873 in one heading (inconsistent with his own table).2, 7, 8, 9, 10

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: T'ung-chih, T'ung Chih, Tung-che (Gulland).

Where the reign’s name appears

Besides its marks, the reign’s name is written on other dated things: coins, inscriptions and dedications.

Later items with this mark

None documented.6

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
