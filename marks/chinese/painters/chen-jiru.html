    In the national survey, 1983 to 1990

    From 1983 to 1990 a group of China’s leading connoisseurs examined the painting and calligraphy in the country’s public collections; their secretary, Lao Jixiong, recorded each work and what was said about it. These are the works by Chen Jiru in Lao’s record, with the collection that held each, the group’s verdict and the remarks of named members. The verdicts are theirs; Heirloom repeats them and does not judge any item. Titles and remarks are translated by Heirloom, with the original Chinese beside each; where the source text is unclear, a translator’s note says so.

    In the Qing imperial catalogs

    The Qianlong emperor had his court catalog the painting and calligraphy in the palaces: 石渠寶笈 (secular works) and 祕殿珠林 (Buddhist and Daoist works), in 1744–45, with further series in 1793 and 1816. These 2 entries are recorded under Chen Jiru’s name, with the hall where each was kept and, in the first series, the court’s grade. A listing means the emperor owned an item with that title; it does not show that any particular scroll is that item.
