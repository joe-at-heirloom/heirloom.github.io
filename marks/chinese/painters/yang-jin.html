    Yang Jin 楊晉

    楊晉
    Name
    Yang Jin 楊晉
    Dates
    1644–1728 清順治元年——雍正六年（公元1644—1728年）
    Other names
    子和 Zihe; 子鶴 Zihe; 西亭 Xiting (West pavilion); 谷林樵客 Gulin qiaoke (Woodcutter of Valley Grove); 野鶴 Yehe (Wild crane); 鶴道人 He daoren (Crane Daoist); 水村 Shuicun (Water village); 志學老人 Zhixue laoren (Old man set on learning); 雪閣移梅人 Xuege yi mei ren (Man who transplants plums from the snow pavilion); 虞山野老 Yushan yelao (Old rustic of Yushan); 虞山樵子 Yushan qiaozi (Woodcutter of Yushan)
    Native place
    江蘇常熟 Jiangsu, Changshu
    Known for
    山水 landscape, 牛, 人物 figures, 肖像 portraits, 花鳥 birds and flowers, 草蟲 insects
    Role
    painter

    Checked against Wikidata Q16240012: the years agree.

    Seals and signatures

    The 127 seals and signatures the Shanghai Museum’s book records for Yang Jin, numbered as in the book (entry 939, from p. 1273), each with its reading, the work it is on, and that work’s date where known. Seals come first and signatures after. A seal a person stamped on someone else’s work, as an owner, is marked as a collector’s seal.

    In the national survey, 1983 to 1990

    From 1983 to 1990 a group of China’s leading connoisseurs examined the painting and calligraphy in the country’s public collections; their secretary, Lao Jixiong, recorded each work and what was said about it. These are the works by Yang Jin in Lao’s record, with the collection that held each, the group’s verdict and the remarks of named members. The verdicts are theirs; Heirloom repeats them and does not judge any item. Titles and remarks are translated by Heirloom, with the original Chinese beside each; where the source text is unclear, a translator’s note says so.

    In the Qing imperial catalogs

    The Qianlong emperor had his court catalog the painting and calligraphy in the palaces: 石渠寶笈 (secular works) and 祕殿珠林 (Buddhist and Daoist works), in 1744–45, with further series in 1793 and 1816. These 6 entries are recorded under Yang Jin’s name, with the hall where each was kept and, in the first series, the court’s grade. A listing means the emperor owned an item with that title; it does not show that any particular scroll is that item.
