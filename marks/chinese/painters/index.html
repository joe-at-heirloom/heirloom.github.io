    Where this comes from

    The people, their names and their seals come from the Shanghai Museum’s 《中國書畫家印鑑款識》 (1987), the standard Chinese reference, which photographed most of its seals and signatures directly from the original works. Dates are checked against Wikidata, and where the two differ each page says so. Works examined in China’s national survey of 1983 to 1990, with the specialists’ verdicts and remarks, come from Lao Jixiong’s record of it.

    A seal or a signature names the person it claims to. Seals and signatures were copied as readily as reign marks, and Heirloom never says whether a work is genuine.
