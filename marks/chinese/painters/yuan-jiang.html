    Seals and signatures

    The 18 seals and signatures the Shanghai Museum’s book records for Yuan Jiang, numbered as in the book (entry 532, from p. 720), each with its reading, the work it is on, and that work’s date where known. Seals come first and signatures after. A seal a person stamped on someone else’s work, as an owner, is marked as a collector’s seal.

    In the national survey, 1983 to 1990

    From 1983 to 1990 a group of China’s leading connoisseurs examined the painting and calligraphy in the country’s public collections; their secretary, Lao Jixiong, recorded each work and what was said about it. These are the works by Yuan Jiang in Lao’s record, with the collection that held each, the group’s verdict and the remarks of named members. The verdicts are theirs; Heirloom repeats them and does not judge any item. Titles and remarks are translated by Heirloom, with the original Chinese beside each; where the source text is unclear, a translator’s note says so.
