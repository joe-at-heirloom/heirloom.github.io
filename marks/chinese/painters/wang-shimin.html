    In the national survey, 1983 to 1990

    From 1983 to 1990 a group of China’s leading connoisseurs examined the painting and calligraphy in the country’s public collections; their secretary, Lao Jixiong, recorded each work and what was said about it. These are the works by Wang Shimin in Lao’s record, with the collection that held each, the group’s verdict and the remarks of named members. The verdicts are theirs; Heirloom repeats them and does not judge any item. Titles and remarks are translated by Heirloom, with the original Chinese beside each; where the source text is unclear, a translator’s note says so.

    • Southern Mountain, hanging scroll 南山圖軸真迹 genuine

      Capital Museum, Beijing 首都博物館 · ink and color on silk · inscribed “癸亥（天啓三年，1623,32 歲）六月大師相岵翁

      按：此鑫是爲岵翁祝壽之作，岵翁爲大司馬，王時敏

    In the Qing imperial catalogs

    The Qianlong emperor had his court catalog the painting and calligraphy in the palaces: 石渠寶笈 (secular works) and 祕殿珠林 (Buddhist and Daoist works), in 1744–45, with further series in 1793 and 1816. These 18 entries are recorded under Wang Shimin’s name, with the hall where each was kept and, in the first series, the court’s grade. A listing means the emperor owned an item with that title; it does not show that any particular scroll is that item.
