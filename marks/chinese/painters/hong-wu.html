    Hong Wu 弘旿

    弘旿
    Name
    Hong Wu 弘旿
    Dates
    清乾隆——嘉慶間（約公元十八世紀）
    Other names
    仲升 Zhongsheng; 一如居士 Yiru jushi (Lay Buddhist of one suchness); 杏村農 Xingcun nong (Farmer of Apricot Village); 卓亭 Zhuoting (Lofty pavilion); 恕齋 Shuzhai (Studio of forgiveness); 瑤華道人 Yaohua daoren (Daoist of Jade Bloom); 醉迂 Zuiyu (Drunken pedant); 醉墨軒主 Zuimo xuan zhu (Master of the Drunken Ink Pavilion)
    Ethnicity
    滿族
    Known for
    山水 landscape, 花卉及篆書, 隸書 clerical script
    Role
    painter, calligrapher

    The book gives a period, not years. See Wikidata Q19852519.

    Seals and signatures

    The 155 seals and signatures the Shanghai Museum’s book records for Hong Wu, numbered as in the book (entry 157, from p. 233), each with its reading, the work it is on, and that work’s date where known. Seals come first and signatures after. A seal a person stamped on someone else’s work, as an owner, is marked as a collector’s seal.

    In the national survey, 1983 to 1990

    From 1983 to 1990 a group of China’s leading connoisseurs examined the painting and calligraphy in the country’s public collections; their secretary, Lao Jixiong, recorded each work and what was said about it. These are the works by Hong Wu in Lao’s record, with the collection that held each, the group’s verdict and the remarks of named members. The verdicts are theirs; Heirloom repeats them and does not judge any item. Titles and remarks are translated by Heirloom, with the original Chinese beside each; where the source text is unclear, a translator’s note says so.
