Thomas Moran's signature, 1862 to 1918

16 close-ups of Thomas Moran's signature (American (born in England), 1837–1926), from works dated 1862–1918, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

After his 1871 journey to Yellowstone, Moran began signing paintings with a 'TYM' monogram, for Thomas 'Yellowstone' Moran.

How Thomas Moran signed

  • The National Gallery of Art reads the monogram on Green River Cliffs (1881) as the letters T, Y (for Yellowstone) and M joined together, followed by 'ORAN'.
  • For further verification, Moran sometimes added his own thumbprint in paint; on Zoroaster Peak (1918) a slender thumbprint sits beside the TYM monogram at lower right.
  • Before Yellowstone he signed in full: a London drawing of 1862 is signed 'Thomas Moran', and The Juniata, Evening (1864) reads 'THOS.MORAN.1864 / OP.8.', with an opus number.
  • On his etchings he used a plain 'TM' monogram in the plate and signed impressions 'T. Moran' in pencil.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Thomas Moran sign paintings?

After his 1871 journey to Yellowstone, Moran began signing paintings with a 'TYM' monogram, for Thomas 'Yellowstone' Moran.

Did Thomas Moran always sign the same way?

No. The 16 examples on this page, from 1862–1918, show TM monogram in plate; pencil signature, TYM monogram signature, TYM monogram signature and date, TYM monogram signature with 'N.A.' (National Academician) and date. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Thomas Moran?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Thomas Moran is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
