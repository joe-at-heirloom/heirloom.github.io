Hongxian mark 洪憲

The Hongxian reign of the Hongxian empire ran 1916. Its mark reads 洪憲年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Porcelain actually made for Yuan's court (supervised at Jingdezhen by Guo Baochang 郭葆昌) is said to bear the hall mark 居仁堂製 (Jurentang) rather than a Hongxian reign mark; a 13-item Jurentang set given by Yuan's daughter is in the Suzhou Museum.1, 2 Hongxian-marked porcelain is overwhelmingly later: dealers produced 洪憲年製 / 洪憲御製 marked wares after Yuan's death in 1916, and the Met dates its Hongxian-marked pair of vases to the 1930s-40s. Jurentang marks were also used on Republic-period wares.1, 2, 3, 4

The Hongxian mark

  • 洪憲年製 · 4 characters, regular script. Most examples are later (see later_copies_note).
  • 洪憲御製 · 4 characters, regular script. Variant named by the Chinese source; also largely later.
1, 2, 5

Years: 1916. Era name proclaimed 31 December 1915 for the year 1916, used from 1 January 1916 and abandoned 22 March 1916 (83 days). Gotheborg labels the period 1915-16.1, 6, 7

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Hung-hsien.

Hongxian marks are almost always later

Hongxian-marked porcelain is overwhelmingly later: dealers produced 洪憲年製 / 洪憲御製 marked wares after Yuan's death in 1916, and the Met dates its Hongxian-marked pair of vases to the 1930s-40s. Jurentang marks were also used on Republic-period wares.1, 2, 3, 4

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
