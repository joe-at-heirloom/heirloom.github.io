Gustav Klimt's signature, 1883 to 1912

9 close-ups of Gustav Klimt's signature (Austrian, 1862–1918), from works dated 1883–1912, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

On the portrait of Gertrud Loew (1902) Klimt placed his signature and the date "1902" inside two green squares reminiscent of the signature stamps on Japanese woodcuts.

How Gustav Klimt signed

  • On the portrait of Emilie Flöge (1902/03) he inscribed two square signets with "Gustav Klimt 1902".
  • He added the signature and the date 1904 to the portrait of Hermine Gallia only after it had been exhibited, in what the Klimt Foundation calls his "already familiar square signet" at the upper right.
  • On drawings he often signed in capitals stacked on two lines, writing a Roman V for U: "GVSTAV / KLIMT".
  • Drawings that left his studio after his death in 1918 often carry the estate stamp "GVSTAV / KLIMT / NACHLASS" (Lugt 1575) rather than a signature.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Gustav Klimt sign paintings?

On the portrait of Gertrud Loew (1902) Klimt placed his signature and the date "1902" inside two green squares reminiscent of the signature stamps on Japanese woodcuts.

Did Gustav Klimt always sign the same way?

No. The 9 examples on this page, from 1883–1912, show estate stamp (Lugt 1575), full name in capitals on two lines, initial, surname and date, square signet with date, beside a second painted square. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Gustav Klimt?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Gustav Klimt is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
