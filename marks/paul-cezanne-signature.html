Paul Cézanne's signature, 1866 to 1876

3 close-ups of Paul Cézanne's signature (French, 1839–1906), from works dated 1866–1876, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Cézanne rarely signed or dated his work, so curators date much of it from style and details such as dress and furnishings.

How Paul Cézanne signed

  • He signed his name without an accent: 'Cezanne'.
  • Of the National Gallery of Art's Cézannes, only three paintings carry a signature, all from 1866–c. 1876; the 1873 House of Père Lacroix is signed and dated 'P. Cezanne 73.'
  • His sketchbook drawings at the National Gallery of Art are unsigned; the numbers on them were added by later hands.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Paul Cézanne sign paintings?

Cézanne rarely signed or dated his work, so curators date much of it from style and details such as dress and furnishings.

Did Paul Cézanne always sign the same way?

No. The 3 examples on this page, from 1866–1876, show initial and surname, initial, surname and date, surname. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Paul Cézanne?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Paul Cézanne is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
