Omega serial number lookup

Enter the serial number engraved on your Omega's movement to see the year the published lists give it, with watches from the same block whose dates are known from Omega's archive extracts or auction records.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

An Omega's serial number is engraved on the movement, usually seven or eight digits, and gives the year the movement was made to within a year or two: 13,500,000 is about 1952, 25,000,000 about 1967. Omega publishes no table; two lists circulate, and watches with known dates check them. Numbers above 56 million, from about 1995, can't be dated this way.

Look up a movement serial

Use the number engraved on the movement, not the reference stamped inside the case back.

Where the serial is, and how to read it safely

On an Omega made before the 1990s the serial is engraved on the movement, usually on a plate or bridge, and the case back has to come off to see it. Screw-down and snap-on backs need the right tool; a slip scratches the case or the movement, and a water-resistant watch needs its seal checked after. If you aren't used to it, ask a watchmaker to open it and photograph the movement for you.

From about 1991 Omega also engraved the serial on the outside of the case back or on a lug, so a modern Omega can be read without opening it. Omega's warranty card carries the serial too.

The other marks on the movement

Beside the serial, the movement carries the maker's name and the caliber number, often by the Omega sign. The caliber number names the movement's design and is shared by every movement of that type, so it narrows the years a watch can be from but does not date it.

Serial or reference?

The serial belongs to one movement. The reference names the model: on vintage watches it is stamped inside the case back, in forms such as 2915-1, 165.024 or ST 145.022-69, and later Omega used longer product codes with dots, such as 311.30.42.30.01.005. Inside the case back there may also be the case maker's own number, a separate series. Only the movement serial is dated here.

Read these before you rely on a year

  • The lists are approximate, to a year or two at best for ordinary movements. Omega numbered movements in blocks and did not always use them in time order, and a watch can have been sold years after its movement was made.
  • The two online lists are not independent from 1944 on: one is largely a re-edit of the other. Dated auction lots (Omega Extracts quoted by Phillips, Christie's, Sotheby's and Bonhams, and the made or sold dates in Antiquorum's 2007 Omegamania sale) are the independent check: they agree within a year or two from about 1895 to the mid-1980s, except that serials from about 10.5 to 13.5 million (the late 1940s and early 1950s) were made one to three years later than the lists say.
  • Chronograph and other special movements were numbered in separate blocks and can be years off the list: 4,636,204 was sold in 1907, the 9.37-9.39 million chronograph block was used from about 1932 to 1941, and Speedmaster caliber 1861 movements numbered 48 million were sold as late as 1998.
  • Omega's 1994 centenary watches, clocks and table chronometers, and prototypes are outside the watch-movement sequence.
  • From about 1991 the serial is also engraved on the case back or a lug, and from about 1995 Omega allotted number blocks to particular calibers, so a high number is not a later watch: dated watches made from 2000 to 2005 carry 80.1, 59.6, 58.4, 80.7, 80.8 and 81.1 million. This table stops at 55,999,999; later serials can't be dated from the number alone.
  • 51,000,000 is dated by one list only, and 52,000,000 was not used; neither is given a year here.
  • Nothing was interpolated between rows. Lists that spread the year markers evenly over the years between them (Dumarko, Libas Collective) were not used.
  • A serial dates a movement. It does not show that a watch is genuine or original: movements, dials and cases were replaced in service and in later assembly.

Questions

Where is the serial number on an Omega watch?

On most Omega watches made before the 1990s it is engraved on the movement, the works inside, so the case back has to come off. Omega says current watches carry it on the back or on a lug, and vintage watches inside the case back and on the movement. Opening a water-resistant case is a job for a watchmaker.

How accurate is an Omega serial number date?

To a year or two for ordinary movements from about 1895 to the mid-1980s. Movements were numbered in blocks and sometimes used out of order, chronographs had blocks of their own, and a watch could be sold years after its movement was made. Watches with known dates show the late 1940s and early 1950s ran one to three years later than the lists.

Why can't my Omega's serial be dated?

From about 1995 Omega gave blocks of numbers to particular movements, so a higher number is not always a later watch. For those, and for any watch where the exact date matters, Omega's Extract from the Archives gives the production date from its own records.

What is the difference between the serial and the reference number?

The serial is unique to one movement or watch. The reference, such as 2915-1, 165.024 or ST 145.022, names the model and is stamped inside the case back; thousands of watches share it. A number stamped inside the case back can also be the case maker's own number, which these lists don't date.

Does the serial show my Omega is genuine?

No. A serial dates a movement. Numbers can be copied, and movements, dials and cases were replaced in service. Heirloom never decides whether a watch is genuine.
