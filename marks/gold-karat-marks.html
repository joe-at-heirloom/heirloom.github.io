Gold karat marks: what 585, 750 and 375 mean

The numbers stamped on gold give its fineness, in karats or in parts per thousand. Here is how to read them, and the marks that mean plated or filled.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

585 on gold means 585 parts of pure gold per thousand: 14 karat. 750 is 18 karat, 375 is 9 karat, 916 is 22 karat and 999 is pure gold. A karat is one 24th; parts per thousand say the same thing in thousandths. GF, RGP, HGE and a fraction such as 1/20 mean a gold layer over another metal.

What the numbers on gold mean

A karat counts parts of pure gold out of 24, by weight: 24 karat is pure gold, 18 karat is 18 parts gold in 24. A three-figure number counts the same thing in parts per thousand: 750 is 750 parts gold in 1,000, which is 18 karat. To turn karats into thousandths, divide by 24 and multiply by 1,000.

Why 585 and not 583

14 divided by 24 is 0.5833, so the exact figure for 14 karat is 583. The usual stamp, and the legal 14 karat standard in the UK and India, is 585, slightly above it. Both are 14 karat. 583 appears on some older gold, including gold marked in the Soviet Union:

Karat marks in the US

American gold is usually marked in karats: 10K, 14K, 18K. Since 1906 the National Gold and Silver Stamping Act has made it an offense to ship gold marked finer than it is. Today a gold item may fall short of its mark by no more than 3 parts per thousand when tested without solder, or 7 parts per thousand tested whole with its solder (3 for watch cases and flatware). Before the law changed in 1976 (in force 1981), the allowance was half a karat, or one karat with solder, so older gold can test a little below its stamp.

Since 1961, whoever stamps a quality mark such as 14K must also stamp their name or a registered trademark next to it, at least as large. A karat mark with no maker's mark beside it on American gold made after that is a reason to look more closely.

The Federal Trade Commission's guides say the word gold alone means pure gold; an alloy must give its karat right beside the word, as in 14K or 14 karat gold. Until 2018 the guides also said gold under 10 karat should not be called gold at all. The 2018 revision dropped that floor, as long as the karat is shown, but kept a 10 karat minimum for the gold layer on filled, plated and vermeil items.

Plated and filled gold marks

These marks mean a thin layer of gold over another metal, not gold through and through. The karat in them describes the layer only.

Reading 1/20 12K GF: the item is gold filled, the gold layer is 12 karat, and the layer makes up one twentieth of the weight of the metal. Since 12 karat is half gold, pure gold is about 2.5% of the metal's weight.

Under British law, gold below a legal standard may be called gold only with the word plated or rolled, in letters at least as large. In the UK a carat description is taken to mean gold of that fineness, unless it is the weight of a stone.

British gold hallmarks, in brief

British gold is hallmarked like silver, with a maker's mark, a fineness mark and an assay office mark, and usually a date letter. The fineness mark changed over time, which dates the gold roughly before you read the letter:

  • To 1798: gold was 22 carat and carried the lion passant, the same mark as sterling silver.
  • 1798 on: 18 carat gold, marked with a crown and the figure 18.
  • 1844 on: 22 carat gold, marked with a crown and 22; the lion was no longer used on gold.
  • 1854 on: three lower standards, 15, 12 and 9 carat, marked with the carat and its decimal (15 and .625, 12 and .5, 9 and .375) and no crown.
  • 1932: 15 and 12 carat ended and 14 carat began.
  • 1975 on: the Hallmarking Act 1973 replaced carats with parts per thousand: a crown with 375, 585, 750 or 916 on gold made in the UK. Imported gold had the number without the crown.
  • 1999 on: the crown and the date letter became optional. 990 and 999 were added. A UK hallmark now needs only the sponsor's (maker's) mark, the number and the assay office mark. Gold under 1 gram need not be hallmarked.

The convention mark, a balance-scale outline around a number, comes from a treaty signed in Vienna in 1972. Countries that belong to it accept each other's convention-marked gold and silver without marking it again.

Swiss gold watch cases

Gold and silver watch cases sold in Switzerland must be checked and marked by the Swiss precious metals control. A Swiss gold case of the mid-20th century often shows the fineness as 18K above 0.750, beside the control's own mark: a head of Helvetia for 750, a squirrel for 583. The control lists both among its former official marks, used since 1880; it gives no end year.

For the date letter itself, see London, Birmingham, Sheffield and Chester.

Read these before you rely on a mark

  • A number stamped on gold says what the maker claims. Only a test says what the metal is; stamps are faked and worn, and plated items sometimes carry a bare karat mark they shouldn't.
  • The karat in a plated or filled mark is the layer's, not the item's.
  • Tolerances differ by country and period: older American gold could legally test half a karat under its stamp.

Questions

What does 585 mean on gold?

585 means 585 parts of pure gold in every 1,000 parts of the metal: 58.5% gold, which is 14 karat. The exact figure for 14 karat is 583, and some gold is marked that way; both mean 14 karat.

What does 750 mean on gold?

750 means 75% pure gold by weight: 18 karat. It is a legal standard in Britain (since 1975 written 750; before that, 18 with a crown) and in many other countries.

Is 375 gold real gold?

Yes: 375 is 9 karat, 37.5% gold. It has been a legal standard in Britain since 1854.

What does 916 mean on gold?

916 is 22 karat: 91.6% gold. British law writes the standard as 916.6. It is common on gold from India and the Middle East.

Is 1/20 12K GF real gold?

It has real gold on it, but it is not solid gold. GF means gold filled: a 12 karat gold layer bonded over a base metal, making up a twentieth of the metal's weight, so about 2.5% of the metal is pure gold.

What does HGE mean on jewelry?

Heavy gold electroplate: a layer of at least 10 karat gold put on by electroplating, at least 2.5 microns thick, over another metal.
