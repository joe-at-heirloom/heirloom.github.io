Jiaqing mark 嘉慶

The Jiaqing reign of the Qing dynasty ran 1796–1820 (some tables to 1821). Its mark reads 大清嘉慶年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Seal script predominates, continuing Qianlong practice.1 Hobson judged later reign marks rarely borrowed, so they generally indicate date; no common copying documented.1, 2

The Jiaqing mark

  • 大清嘉慶年製 · 6 characters, seal script. Usual form.
  • 大清嘉慶年製 · 6 characters, regular script. Less frequent.
  • 嘉慶御製 · 4 characters, regular script. 'Made by imperial order' variant (Bushell).
1, 3, 4

Years: 1796–1820 (some tables to 1821). 1796-1820; Gulland 1796-1821.2, 3, 5, 6, 7

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Chia-ch'ing, Chia Ch'ing, Kea-king (Gulland).

On imperial wares

Later items with this mark

Hobson judged later reign marks rarely borrowed, so they generally indicate date; no common copying documented.1, 2

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
