Utagawa Hiroshige's signature, 1831 to 1858

12 close-ups of Utagawa Hiroshige's signature (Japanese, 1797–1858), from works dated 1831–1858, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

About a year after taking him into his studio, his master Utagawa Toyohiro granted him the artist name Hiroshige, combining the 'hiro' of Toyohiro with 'shige', a reading of the first character of his given name Jūemon.

How Utagawa Hiroshige signed

  • Museum records list Ichiyūsai and Ichiryūsai among Hiroshige's alternate names; both appear on his prints beside the signature or as seals.
  • On the One Hundred Famous Views of Edo prints the artist's signature, 'Hiroshige ga', sits in a lower box, alongside the publisher's seal (Uoya Eikichi) and censor's seals giving a month and year.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Utagawa Hiroshige sign paintings?

About a year after taking him into his studio, his master Utagawa Toyohiro granted him the artist name Hiroshige, combining the 'hiro' of Toyohiro with 'shige', a reading of the first character of his given name Jūemon.

Did Utagawa Hiroshige always sign the same way?

No. The 12 examples on this page, from 1831–1858, show Hiroshige ga, Hiroshige ga in cartouche, Hiroshige ga with Hiro seal, Hiroshige ga with Ichiryūsai seal. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Utagawa Hiroshige?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Utagawa Hiroshige is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
