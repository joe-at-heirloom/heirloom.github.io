Rolex serial number lookup

Enter the serial from your Rolex's case to see the year the published lists give it, each source's answer where they differ, and watches from the same block whose dates are known.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

A Rolex serial is engraved between the lugs at 6 o'clock, and on later watches on the inner ring at 6 o'clock. Numeric serials run from about 1926 to 1987 in two numberings (the count restarted in 1953–54), then a letter and six digits until about 2010: R is about 1987–88, P about 2000. Since about 2010 serials are random and carry no date.

Look up a serial number

Use the number at 6 o'clock, not the model number at 12. Spaces and commas don't matter.

Where the serial is, and how to read it safely

On a Rolex made before about 2008 the serial is engraved on the case between the lugs at 6 o'clock, where the bracelet or strap hides it. Taking the bracelet off means pushing out its spring bars, and a slipped tool scratches the lugs, so a jeweler or watchmaker is the safer way; they can read the number and fit the bracelet back in a few minutes.

On some 1920s Rolex cases the serial is stamped inside the case back instead, among the maker's and patent stamps. The published lists disagree this early, so a British hallmark in the same back is the better guide to the date.

From about 2005 Rolex also engraved the serial on the rehaut, the ring between the dial and the crystal, at 6 o'clock, and from about 2008 only there. It can be read without opening the watch: tilt it under a bright light and use a magnifier. The warranty card, or the papers that came with the watch, carry the same serial.

Serial or model number?

The serial belongs to one watch. The model number, or reference, names the model, the bezel and the metal; it is engraved between the lugs at 12 o'clock, and on older watches often stamped inside the case back as well. A Datejust 16234 and a Submariner 5513 are references, shared by thousands of watches. Only the serial is dated here.

The name, the crown and the engraved ring

The name and the crown on the dial say who made the watch, and the ROLEX engraving round the inner ring marks a case of about 2004 or later. None of them dates a watch more closely or shows that it is genuine.

Random serials, from about 2010

From about late 2009 or 2010 Rolex replaced the letter series with random serials of eight letters and digits. They aren't issued in order, so no year can be read from one. The warranty card is dated when the watch is sold; for a watch without papers, the date can't be told from the serial.

Read these before you rely on a year

  • Rolex publishes no serial chart and no dates of manufacture. Every list is compiled by collectors and dealers, and most online lists are copies of two: an interpolated chart with no 1950s restart (S), and Bob's Watches' list (A). The independent sources are two books, Dowling and Hess (dated watches, 1928-1965) and Pergola (ranges, 1952-1971), and dated watches sold at auction.
  • Agreement: allowing for the restart, Dowling and Hess, Pergola and Bob's agree with watches dated by a case-back stamp, a hallmark or papers to within about a year from 1933 to 1967. That is the best-supported stretch.
  • 1928-1948 rests on one underlying list: Bob's repeats most of Dowling and Hess's rows, so the two agree by descent, not independently. Three dated watches (1933, 1943 and 1947) fit it. Before 23,969 (about 1928) the lists disagree and no year is given.
  • The restart: Pergola and Bob's put it in 1954, Dowling and Hess late in 1953, and the round-number restart some charts show (999,999 in 1954, then 200,000 in 1955) is made up. Dowling and Hess also date two second-numbering watches, 116,578 and 132,562, to 1953; dated watches near 111,000-113,000 are from 1955-56, so those two rows were not used.
  • 1971-1987 rests on two dealer lists, Bob's (origin not stated) and the interpolated chart, which differ by one to two years; dated papers fit both. Treat these years as within a year or two.
  • Letter era: the three list families start most letters within a year of each other, and the span shown covers all three. A dated warranty card shows when the watch was sold, so it can be a year or more after the letter's year. No dated papers were found for S, A, V or G serials.
  • Random serials (from about late 2009 or 2010): the eight-character serial is not in sequence and can't be dated from the number. The warranty card or the retailer's records give the date of sale.
  • Some early models, the Rolex Prince among them, were numbered in series of their own and are outside this table.
  • A case serial dates the case. It does not show that a watch is genuine or original: serials are copied, and cases, dials, bracelets and movements were replaced in service.
  • Nothing was interpolated between rows. Where a list has no entry for a year (Bob's has none for 1929, 1931, 1949 or 1950), its answer is the whole span between its two entries.

Sources

Each year here is given by at least two of these, shown as a range where they differ, and was checked against the dated watches:

  • James M. Dowling and Jeffrey P. Hess, The Best of Time: Rolex Wristwatches, An Unauthorized History (Schiffer): serial and dated-watch list (source)
  • Carlo Pergola, Rolex Daytona: A Legend Is Born: case-serial ranges by year, 1952-1971 (source)
  • Bob's Watches, Rolex serial numbers (current list) (source)
  • The "Rolex date chart" (28,000 in 1926; called the Specht Sheet by Alan Furman & Co.): undated PDF on a Shopify file server, and the copies at Watches by Timepiece, Watch Collectors UK, Keep The Time, WatchDoctor and Alan Furman (source)
  • Auction lots with an independent date: a case-back date stamp, a British import hallmark, or dated Rolex papers (Phillips, Christie's, Bonhams, Antiquorum; each lot named in 'dated_examples') (source)

Not used: Luxury Bazaar and Phigora (rounded splices of Pergola and Bob's with evenly split letter ranges), Amsterdam Vintage Watches, BeckerTime and The Watch Guys (splices of the lists above, with some values of unknown origin), Expert's Watches (Dowling and Hess plus S). Rolex publishes no serial chart, and no chart issued by Rolex to dealers was found.

Questions

Where is the serial number on a Rolex?

Until about 2008, engraved on the case between the lugs at 6 o'clock, under the bracelet or strap. From about 2005 it is also engraved on the inner ring between the dial and the crystal (the rehaut), at 6 o'clock, and from about 2008 only there. The warranty card carries it too.

Can I find the year of my Rolex from its serial number?

For serials up to about 2010, to within a year or two: enter it above. Numbers below 1,000,000 were used twice, before and after a restart in the 1950s, so the lookup shows both and the model decides. Since about 2010 Rolex has used random serials that carry no date; the warranty card shows when the watch was sold.

What is the difference between a Rolex serial number and the model number?

The serial is unique to one watch and sits at 6 o'clock. The model number, or reference, names the model, its bezel and its metal (a Datejust 16234, a Submariner 5513) and sits between the lugs at 12 o'clock; on older watches it is often stamped inside the case back too. Thousands of watches share a reference.

Why does my Rolex serial give two different years?

Either because the number was used in both numberings, before and after the 1950s restart, or because the sources put that block in different years. The lookup shows each source's answer rather than choosing one.

Does a Rolex serial number prove the watch is genuine?

No. A serial dates a case; numbers are copied, and cases, dials and movements were replaced in service. Heirloom never decides whether a watch is genuine. If that matters, Rolex or an independent watchmaker can examine it.

Can a serial number tell me my Rolex's value?

No. The year is one fact about the watch; the model, its condition, its parts and what comparable watches have actually sold for matter far more. A record is complete without a price.
