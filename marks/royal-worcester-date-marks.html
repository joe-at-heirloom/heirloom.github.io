Royal Worcester date marks, 1862 to 1963

The standard Royal Worcester mark carries a year code: a letter, a count of dots, a star or a shape. Choose what yours shows and count.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Royal Worcester dated its standard printed mark (the crowned circle) from 1862 to 1963: a letter until 1890, then dots added one a year, a star from 1916, shapes from 1928, three linked circles with dots from 1933, and W (or R in a circle) with dots from 1950. A mark with 12 dots beside the crown is 1903.

Decode a Royal Worcester date mark

Choose what you see under the crowned circle, then type the letter or count the dots. Count every dot, on both sides of the crown and below it.

What the mark looks like

The standard printed mark, a crowned circle with the factory's cypher and 51 in the middle, with the year letter underneath. All three items are in the Met, which dates them 1878, 1879 and about 1886, the years the table gives for N, P and X. On the third, a registered design number printed just below (not shown) is from 1885, which fits. Photographs: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access (public domain), via Wikimedia Commons (moon flask, LC-2018.62.72, vase, LC-2018.62.103, vase, LC-2018.62.108), cropped by Heirloom.

No openly licensed photograph of a dated mark from after 1890 (dots, star, shapes or W) has been found yet, so those years have no picture here.

Read these before you rely on a year

  • A date code only means something next to the right factory mark (impressed WEDGWOOD, impressed MINTON(S), the Royal Worcester printed mark, the Rookwood RP monogram, the Sevres interlaced Ls). A code alone does not identify the maker, and several of these systems were copied or forged (Minton by smaller makers; Sevres very widely).
  • The codes give a single year only when read exactly. Letters that look alike (I/J, U/V, O/Q, C/G) and blurred or glaze-filled impressions are the main source of error. When a reading is uncertain, report the candidate years.
  • Applies to the standard Worcester Royal Porcelain Co. printed mark (crowned circle with interlaced Ws and a crescent), mainly on bone china. R1 says earthenware (crownware) and Vitreous wares sometimes follow the dot code, and hard porcelain rarely has any code.
  • 1862 to 1875: specific year indications are rare. When present they are the last two digits of the year below the mark, e.g. 75 = 1875 (R1). R2 says two-digit years were used 1862 to 1867, and from 1867 to 1877 either the two digits or the letter.
  • Crown position: from 1876 the crown sits down on the circle; before that it is slightly above or perched on it (R1, R2). This is a quick check on letters A to K.
  • Letters 1867 to 1890 are upper case, except lower-case a = 1890. F, J and Q were not used, and O comes after Z (1889) (R1, R2, R3). Case matters: 'a' = 1890 but 'A' = 1867.
  • Dots: 1891 has 'Royal Worcester England' round the circle and no dots. Each later year adds one dot, first beside the crown (left, then right, alternately; 6 and 6 by 1903), then below the circle (1 in 1904 up to 12 in 1915). Year = 1891 + total dots (R1, R2, R3). Count every dot. R3 shows a real 1902 mark with the dots on the wrong sides.
  • Star period: 1916 has a small star or asterisk below the mark. Year = 1916 + the number of dots with the star (1917 = star and 1 dot ... 1927 = star and 11 dots) (R1, R2, R4). R3 warns that smudged dots can look like the star. R2 says some items kept the old dot plates for a few years after 1916, so a count above 24 with no star may be 1916 or later.
  • 1928 to 1932 shapes printed below the mark: 1928 open square (R1 says 'oblong'), 1929 open diamond, 1930 division sign (÷), 1931 two linked circles, 1932 three linked circles. From 1933, year = 1932 + dots beside the three circles, up to 9 dots in 1941 (R1, R2, R4). A single circle was never used (R5).
  • 1942 to 1948 is disputed. R1 (the factory museum): no date code was used. R4: the mark was 'left unchanged', so three circles and 9 dots may appear on 1941 to 1948 wares. R2's table: three circles plus 10 to 16 dots (1942 to 1948), though R2's text also says no dating system was used. So three circles and 9 dots means 1941 or later (possibly to 1948). Ten or more dots with three circles fits 1942 to 1948 only on R2's table.
  • 1949 = V; 1950 = W; 1951 to 1963 = W plus one dot a year, so year = 1950 + dots (R1, R2, R4). R2 says that after 1956 the W was nearly always replaced by an R in a circle with the same dot count (e.g. R-in-circle with 9 dots = 1959). R1 says 1956 is 'W or R in a circle' with 6 dots.
  • After 1963 there is no date code. From the mid-1960s tableware stamps give the year the PATTERN was introduced, not the year the item was made (R1, R2). Do not read those dates as manufacture dates.
  • Later systems (outside this table): 1988 M in a diamond, 1989 N in a diamond, and from 1990 a gray lithographer number with a year suffix (R1, R2).
  • A bare 1891-style mark with no dots does not always mean 1891. R5 describes a Pekin tyg with a no-dot mark but a registered design number of 1913.

Questions

How do I date Royal Worcester by the dots?

From 1892 one dot was added each year, first beside the crown, then below the circle: the year is 1891 plus the total number of dots, up to 1915. From 1916 a star appears, and the year is 1916 plus any dots beside it.

Why does a letter give two years?

V and W were used twice: V for 1884 and 1949, W for 1885 and 1950. The crown's position, the wording around the mark and the style of the ware tell them apart.

What about a date in the pattern stamp on later tableware?

From the mid-1960s, tableware stamps give the year the pattern was introduced, not the year the item was made.
