Alphonse Mucha's signature, 1896 to 1909

7 close-ups of Alphonse Mucha's signature (Czech, 1860–1939), from works dated 1896–1909, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Mucha's posters carry his signature printed in the lithographic stone, often with the printer's name elsewhere on the sheet; Princeton's Job poster of 1897 is 'Signed in stone, lower right: Mucha' with F. Champenois, Paris, at lower left.

How Alphonse Mucha signed

  • On the National Gallery of Art's Biscuits Lefèvre-Utile poster (1896) 'Mucha' is printed on the stone at lower left, with 'F. Champenois. Paris.' at bottom center.
  • After the success of his Gismonda poster for Sarah Bernhardt, Mucha signed an exclusive contract with the printer Champenois for commercial and decorative posters.

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Alphonse Mucha sign paintings?

Mucha's posters carry his signature printed in the lithographic stone, often with the printer's name elsewhere on the sheet; Princeton's Job poster of 1897 is 'Signed in stone, lower right: Mucha' with F. Champenois, Paris, at lower left.

Did Alphonse Mucha always sign the same way?

No. The 7 examples on this page, from 1896–1909, show estate (bequest) stamp, surname and date, painted, surname and date, printed, with a credit line, surname, printed. Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Alphonse Mucha?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Alphonse Mucha is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
