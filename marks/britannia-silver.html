Britannia silver: the 958 standard and its marks

Britannia silver has more silver in it than sterling. Here is why it began, how it is marked, and how to tell it from Britannia metal.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Britannia silver is 958 parts silver in 1000, against sterling's 925. It was the compulsory standard for English silver from 1697 to 1720, marked with a seated figure of Britannia and a lion's head erased, and has been an optional standard since. Britannia metal is something else: a tin alloy with no silver.

Britannia silver is a higher standard than sterling: 958 parts of silver in 1000 against sterling's 925. From 1697 to 1720 it was the only standard English silversmiths could use, and it has been allowed alongside sterling ever since.

Britannia-standard marks on a London silver chocolate pot: the maker's mark, the figure of Britannia, the lion's head erased and the date letter. Photograph: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access (public domain), cut out and toned by Heirloom.

Why it began

By the 1690s so much coin was being melted down for plate that silver coin had grown scarce. An Act of 1696 raised the standard for wrought plate above the coinage, so that coin could no longer be turned straight into plate. The new standard was 11 ounces 10 pennyweights of silver in the pound troy, against the sterling coin standard of 11 ounces 2 pennyweights.

The marks

  • The figure of Britannia in place of the lion passant.
  • A lion's head erased in place of London's leopard's head.
  • The maker's mark as the first two letters of the maker's surname.
  • A date letter, as before. See the London date letters.

When it was used

  • 1697 to 1720, compulsory. Jackson gives the new marks as in use from 27 March 1697 until 1 June 1720. The Act restoring sterling was passed in 1719, which is the year Chaffers gives; it took effect in 1720.
  • 1720 on, optional. Sterling came back because the higher standard was too soft for hard wear, but makers kept the choice of Britannia, and still have it.
  • Today. The UK standard is written 958 (958.4 in the Act), and the figure of Britannia is an optional mark beside the number.

Britannia silver is not Britannia metal

Britannia metal is an alloy of tin with antimony and a little copper, close to pewter, with no silver in it. It was used for spoons, forks and other tableware. The shared name causes confusion: Britannia silver is marked with the figure of Britannia or 958, and Britannia metal carries neither.

Questions

What is Britannia silver?

Silver of at least 958 parts in 1000, a higher standard than sterling (925). It was compulsory in England from 1697 to 1720 and has been an optional standard since.

What does the Britannia mark on silver mean?

The figure of Britannia means the silver is of the Britannia standard, 958. Between 1697 and 1720 it appears with a lion's head erased in place of the leopard's head.

Is Britannia silver better than sterling?

It has more silver in it, which also makes it softer. That softness is why sterling was restored as the usual standard in 1720.

Is Britannia metal silver?

No. Britannia metal is a tin alloy, close to pewter, with no silver in it.
