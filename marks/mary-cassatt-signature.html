Mary Cassatt's signature, 1875 to 1905

15 close-ups of Mary Cassatt's signature (American, 1844–1926), from works dated 1875–1905, in date order. Each is cut from a museum photograph of the work and links to the museum's record.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Cassatt signed her paintings and pastels with her full name, 'Mary Cassatt', usually at the lower right and without a date.

How Mary Cassatt signed

  • An early portrait of her nephew is inscribed rather than signed: 'Eddy / from / Aunt Mary' (1875).
  • Her 1890–91 color prints are pencil-signed below the image with a note that she printed them with M. Leroy in an edition of 25: 'Imprimée par l'artiste et M. Leroy / Mary Cassatt / (25 épreuves)'.
  • Some impressions are only initialled 'M.C' in pencil, and some carry a stamped 'MC' monogram.
  • Signatures scratched into a printing plate or picked up on a counterproof read backwards, as on The Crocheting Lesson (c. 1902).

What a signature can't tell you

Signatures were copied, added to unsigned works and printed onto reproductions. A signature that looks like these is a lead, not an answer, and Heirloom never decides whether a work is genuine. For how to read a signature and where to look one up, see artist signatures.

Questions

How did Mary Cassatt sign paintings?

Cassatt signed her paintings and pastels with her full name, 'Mary Cassatt', usually at the lower right and without a date.

Did Mary Cassatt always sign the same way?

No. The 15 examples on this page, from 1875–1905, show full name, full name (graphite), full name (pencil, print), full name (reversed, counterproof). Signatures change with age, medium and the size of the work.

Does a matching signature mean a work is by Mary Cassatt?

No. A signature is the easiest part of a work to copy or add. Whether a work is by Mary Cassatt is a question for a specialist in the artist's work and, where one exists, the catalogue raisonné.
