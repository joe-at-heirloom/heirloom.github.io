Hull pottery marks

Hull marked its ware with an H, then with its name and a pattern code, then with script and line names after the 1950 flood. The mark and the code together tell you roughly when.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

The A. E. Hull Pottery began in Crooksville, Ohio, in 1905, was wrecked by a flood in 1950, reopened as the Hull Pottery Company in 1952 and closed in 1986 1, 2, 3. An H in a circle is an early mark; Hull Art U.S.A. with a pattern code belongs to the matte lines of the 1940s; script Hull and line names such as Tokay come after 1950 2, 3, 4.

Who made Hull pottery

In 1905 Addis E. Hull and two partners left the Globe Stoneware Company and organized the A. E. Hull Pottery Company in Crooksville, Ohio 1, 2. It took over the Acme Pottery, whose plant became Hull's plant number 2 1, 3. It made stoneware and kitchenware, and from 1917 art pottery for gift and department stores 1, 3.

After A. E. Hull died in 1930, his son ran the firm until 1937, when he left to manage the Shawnee Pottery in Zanesville and Gerald Watts took over 1, 2.

On the night of June 16, 1950, a flood on Black Fork creek swamped the plant and its red-hot kilns exploded 1, 5. The plant stood idle until 1952, when it reopened as the Hull Pottery Company 2, 3. It closed in 1986 2, 3.

Hull marks, in order

Where we have no open-license photograph or period-book facsimile of a mark, its card describes it in words. Some Hull items are unmarked 4, 7.

H in a circle, impressed

About 1910 to 1935

A bold H inside a circle, impressed in the base of stoneware, kitchenware and early art ware, often with numbers 2, 3, 4. The Kovels also record a plain A. E. Hull name mark of the 1930s 3.

Hull Art U.S.A. with a pattern code

The 1940s

Hull Art U.S.A. or Hull U.S.A., raised from the mold, with a code for the pattern and the height in inches: W-7-7½ is Wildflower, shape 7, seven and a half inches tall 3, 4.

Red Riding Hood patent marks

From 1943

Hull held the design patent for the Little Red Riding Hood cookie jar, granted in 1943 to Louise Bauer and assigned to the A. E. Hull Pottery Company 6, 8. Jars are marked Hull Ware Little Red Riding Hood Patent Applied For, or Pat. Des. No. 135889 USA, or not at all 6, 7.

Hull in script

From about 1950

Hull in script with U.S.A., raised from the mold, with the pattern code, as on the Woodland line 3, 4.

Hull U.S.A. impressed, and Oven Proof

The 1950s and 1960s

Hull and USA impressed with a shape number on planters and kitchen ware, and Oven Proof marks on the 1950s kitchen lines 3, 4. Lower-case hull marks with the registered and copyright signs belong to the later years; the sources do not agree on when they began 2, 4.

Line names instead of Hull

From the 1950s

Some later lines carry only their own name with U.S.A.: Regal from about 1952, Tokay from 1958, Imperial and Coronet from 1960 3, 4.

Look-alike marks to be careful with

  • Red Riding Hood jars marked McCoy are not Hull's. The columnist Harry Rinker reported that they were made by the Tennessee man who registered the McCoy name in the 1990s 8, a registration the trademark office records from 1993 to 1999 9.
  • An H in a diamond is shown as a Hull mark in the Kovels' dictionary, but Lehner says Hull never used it and that it was registered by the Carrollton Pottery 2, 3.
  • The trademark office records a Hull Pottery logo registered by an unrelated business from 2004 to 2015, after the pottery closed 10. It is not a mark of the old firm.
  • We found no published record of reproductions carrying a Hull mark.

Heirloom never decides whether an item is genuine. A mark says what it claims; a specialist who can handle the item is the one to ask.

Where the sources disagree

  • When Hull took over Acme: 1907 in the Crooksville history 1, after about two years, so about 1905, in the Kovels 3.
  • The H in a diamond, as above 2, 3.
  • The flood: the night of June 16 to 17, 1950, in the newspapers of June 17 5; some later accounts give June 19.

Questions

What do the numbers in a Hull mark mean?

On the 1940s matte lines, a letter and two numbers give the pattern, the shape and the height in inches: W-7-7½ is Wildflower, shape 7, seven and a half inches 3, 4.

When did Hull pottery close?

In 1986 2, 3.

What happened to Hull in 1950?

A flood on the night of June 16 swamped the plant and its kilns exploded; it reopened as the Hull Pottery Company in 1952 2, 3, 5.

Did Hull make the Little Red Riding Hood cookie jar?

Hull held the 1943 design patent for it, and jars are marked with Hull Ware or the patent number 6, 7. Who decorated the jars is told differently by different sources.
