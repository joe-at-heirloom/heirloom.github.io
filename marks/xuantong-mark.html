Xuantong mark 宣統

The Xuantong reign of the Qing dynasty ran 1909–1911 (some tables from 1908 and to 1912). Its mark reads 大清宣統年製. Here is how it was written on imperial wares, and how often it was copied.

By Heirloom · Updated November 22, 2026

In short

Short reign with limited output; Hobson calls it 'a blank' in ceramic history. Marked imperial items exist but are uncommon.1, 2, 3 No common later copies are recorded in the sources.3

The Xuantong mark

  • 大清宣統年製 · 6 characters, regular script. The usual imperial form: two columns, without a frame, mostly in underglaze blue.
2, 3, 4

Years: 1909–1911 (some tables from 1908 and to 1912). Era name in use from the lunar New Year of 1909; Puyi abdicated 12 February 1912. Hobson gives 1909-1911, Gotheborg 1909-1912, Burton & Hobson 1910-12, UCSD and Wikipedia (reign) 1908-1912.1, 2, 5

Other spellings in older books and catalogs: Hsüan-t'ung, Hsuan T'ung.

Later items with this mark

None documented.3

A reign mark names the reign it claims. Heirloom never decides whether an item is of that period or genuine; a specialist who can handle it is the one to ask.
