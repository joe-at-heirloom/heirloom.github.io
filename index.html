Why Heirloom

Your money has an app.
What about your things?

Your accounts have an app — balances, history, a total that updates itself. Meanwhile the painting over the mantel, your grandfather's watch, the shelf of first editions: no record of what they are, where they came from, or who they are meant for. Heirloom is that record — one place where the things you own are written down, with their photos and their stories, and an estimate where there is evidence for one.

Most apps assume you know nothing about your things. Collectors know better. Heirloom is built for both, and never makes the collector sit through the beginner flow.

“Photograph it”

Found it in the attic. Inherited it. Just curious.

Take a few photos. The Advisor reads the marks, labels and details it can see, says plainly what it cannot tell, and asks a question only when the answer would change something. An estimate appears when there is evidence for one — many keepsakes have no market value, and that is fine.

“Write it down”

You're the collector. You did the research years ago.

Catalogue it yourself — maker, provenance, condition, your own valuation. Your word is the record, and Heirloom never rewrites it. The Advisor stays out of the way unless you add photos, change what the item is, or ask for a second look or for comparable sales.

What Heirloom does

The record, the story
and the estimate, in one place

The Advisor

Reads what your photos show — marks, labels, materials, condition — and says plainly what it cannot tell. It never decides whether an item is genuine; that question belongs to a specialist you choose. An estimate is offered only when there is evidence for one, and it is labeled as an estimate, never dressed up as an appraisal. Between visits it keeps a short review list rather than a growing backlog: work that finished, what is waiting on you, and changes that affect a decision you already made — each one with a way to deal with it now, later, or not at all.

An overview that adds up

One place that says what you own and what you are counting: “From 18 items”, split into figures backed by recorded sales and figures you entered yourself. Tap any number to see exactly which records are in it. Items with no established value are counted as unknown, never as zero, and currencies are never added together. When you sell, give away or lose something, say so — the record and its history stay, and your total stops counting it.

Your collection

Catalogue, tag, search and filter across everything you own — photos, documents, dimensions, serial numbers and your own notes on each record. Filters for room, person, missing story and unvalued items show what still needs attention, without turning a keepsake into a task.

Research & comparables

When you photograph an item, a search for comparable sales is part of how the Advisor reads it; on a record you write down yourself, nothing runs until you change what it is or ask. When comparable sales can be found, each is shown with its source, its date and whether it sold or was only listed, so you can keep it or set it aside. A research chat covers maker histories and context.

Specialist help, if you ask

For a record that deserves professional eyes, ask for an introduction to an independent appraiser or dealer — for an identification, a valuation, advice on selling, or a written appraisal from an independent professional. You see exactly what will be shared before it goes. Heirloom lists nothing and nothing is put up for sale. No money for a sale changes hands through Heirloom: any sale is between you and the specialist you chose.

Records that outlast you

Note who each item is meant for and who should receive your records. That list is private and grants no one access to your account. When you need to do something with the records, start from the items and say what it is for — an insurance conversation, a family or executor record, or working with someone else. Heirloom names only what is missing for that purpose, lets you go ahead without it, and keeps a record of exactly what left the app, so you can see later what was sent and what has changed since.

Private by design

Your collection is yours. Records are scoped to your account, photos live in private storage, family sharing is by invitation with a named role, and you can export or delete everything from Settings.

Collectors

Document the collection you know inside out. Your attributions, your valuations, your provenance — organized, searchable and ready to export.

Inheritors & families

Grandma's china, the painting from the estate, the watch in the drawer. Find out what is known about them, keep the story of who they came from, and share the record with the family — whether or not any of it has a market value.

Insurance & estate conversations

Bring an inventory, an insurance conversation or a family record to your insurer, attorney or executor. Heirloom prepares it from the items you pick, tells you what is missing for that purpose, and keeps a record of what you sent. The files are yours to hand over; Heirloom does not open your account to an executor or an heir.
